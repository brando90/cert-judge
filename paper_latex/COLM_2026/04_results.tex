\section{Results}

\paragraph{Property-level certification.}
The judge satisfies identity exactly on the P1 suite (\(R^2=1.000\)).
Under controlled perturbations, it shows strong monotonic behavior:
for bug ladders, Pearson \(r=-0.973\) and Spearman \(\rho=-0.985\);
for specification-ablation ladders, Pearson \(r=-0.979\) and Spearman \(\rho=-0.985\).
These trends indicate that the judge tracks directional quality changes rather than only superficial similarity.

\paragraph{Fitting a score mapping to human agreement.}
On a fixed human-labeled panel, the variance-aware trust index correlates with judge-level human alignment.
We observe Spearman correlations of \(0.833\) (average labels), \(0.905\) (pass1), and \(0.738\) (pass2).
These results support the human-agreement hypothesis that higher trust-index values correspond to better human agreement on this suite.

\paragraph{Downstream ranking on \textsc{VeriBench}.}
After certification, we evaluate agent families on \textsc{VeriBench}.
Table~\ref{tab:theorem_quality_table_split} shows that feedback-driven methods outperform baseline prompting.
\textsc{DSPy ReAct} attains the highest average normalized score (\(0.615\)) versus baseline prompting (\(0.470\)).
This demonstrates practical utility: certified judges can provide scalable ranking signals across agent configurations.
