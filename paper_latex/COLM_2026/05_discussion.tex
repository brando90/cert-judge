\section{Discussion, Limitations, and Future Work}

\paragraph{Measurement-science perspective.}
This work argues that judge validation should include explicit behavioral validity checks, not only judge--human correlation.
Property-based certification provides an auditable scaffold for AI evaluation in interactive and rapidly changing agent ecosystems.

\paragraph{Relation to AI Measurement Science.}
For \emph{interactive measurement}, the protocol evaluates judges on end-to-end generated artifacts.
For \emph{strategic optimization}, it surfaces brittle scoring behaviors before benchmark deployment.
For \emph{non-stationarity}, it supports periodic reassessment when models, prompts, or data mixtures drift.
These are not presentation themes; they are validity risks for deployed evaluators.
Our protocol treats them as measurable quantities, which is the central shift from benchmark-only reporting to measurement-science practice.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Our claims are development-set claims.
The trust index is not a universal constant and does not guarantee out-of-domain validity.
The perturbation suite is finite, so unmodeled failure modes can remain.
The human panel is limited in size and annotator diversity, and stronger inter-rater protocols are needed.

\paragraph{Future directions.}
We plan to expand human-agreement assessment with larger and more diverse annotation panels, include explicit adversarial judge-gaming tests, and study adaptive reassessment schedules under temporal drift.
We also plan to release ``judge cards'' that report validity evidence, stress-test coverage, and uncertainty for transparent deployment decisions.
More broadly, we view evaluator cards plus property checks as a candidate standard interface between model builders and measurement stakeholders.
