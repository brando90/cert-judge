\section{Definitions}
\label{sec:defs}

\begin{definition}[A universally trustworthy AI judge]
\label{def:tw}
Let \(B\) be a set of benchmarks and \(A\) a set of agents.
An AI judge $aj \in AJ$ is universally trustworthy if, for every benchmark \(b \in B\) and every agent/solver \(a \in A\),
$aj$ distributionally matches the corresponding \emph{target judge (tj \in TJ)} (e.g., a human, a persona, a strict rubric, a lenient rubric, etc.):
\begin{equation}
\forall b \in B, \forall a \in A, \quad
\hat p_\theta(s \mid x,y^*,\hat{y}{=}a(x), aj)
= \hat p^*_t(s \mid x,y^*,\hat{y}{=}a(x), t)
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Trust Index]
\label{def:ti}
The criterion (needs labels) is the panel correlation
\begin{equation}
\TIm{1}(aj) = \mathrm{Corr}_{i \in \text{panel}}\big(\hat s_{aj}(i),\, s^*_{h_j}(i)\big).
\label{eq:ti}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Proxy Trust Index]
\label{def:pti}
The label-free instrument is the geometric mean of a locked property subset
\begin{equation}
\PTI_p(aj) = \GM\big(P_k(aj) : k \in p\big).
\label{eq:pti}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\section{Preference is not the target}
\label{sec:preference}

This section names what Definition~\ref{def:tw} is \emph{not}.
The object being matched is a judgment---a sensation, a score---under an agreed
frame of reference, not a preference.

\paragraph{Preference means want.}
In these notes, \emph{preference} means a want or a goal, as in human preferences
(what someone would choose). It does not mean the scoring target, the rubric, or
the human judge \(h_j\).

That is why the contrast ``I prefer \(X\), but \(Y\) is the trustworthy answer''
is the wrong sentence. Preference and trustworthiness are not two answers to the
same question. Preference is a goal. Trustworthiness is whether the judge's
induced score distribution matches the agreed target (a person, a strict or
lenient rubric, an objective measure of a behavior) once the persona
precondition \(t_j = a_j\) is fixed.

\paragraph{Subjectivity after a frame of reference.}
The claim is that residual subjectivity does not survive an agreed frame of
reference. Given \(h_j = (\text{person}, \pi)\)---or any other named target---the
question is distributional match, not taste. Feelings and other judgments in that
frame are still modeled with probability theory: they are \(P_H(s \mid \cdot, h_j)\)
in Definition~\ref{def:tw}.
