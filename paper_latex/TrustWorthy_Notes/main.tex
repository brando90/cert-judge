\paragraph{Notation note.}
\(\hat p_T\) denotes the target-judge pdf/pmf; the hat indicates that the estimate is finite-sample.
We write \(\hat p_T\) (or \(\hat p_H\) when the target is human) and \(\hat p_{AJ}\) for the AI-judge side.

\section{Behaviors and agents}

\begin{definition}[Universally trustworthy AI judge]
\label{def:tw}
Let \(B\) be a set of benchmarks and \(A\) a set of agents.
An AI judge \(aj \in AJ\) is universally trustworthy if, for every benchmark \(b \in B\) and every agent/solver \(a \in A\),
\(aj\) distributionally matches the corresponding \emph{target judge} (\(tj \in TJ\))
(e.g., a human, a persona, a strict rubric, a lenient rubric, etc.):
\[
\forall b \in B,\; \forall a \in A,\quad
\text{if } t_j = a_j
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\hat p_{AJ}\bigl(s \mid x,\, \hat{y}(c),\, y^*,\, \theta_j\bigr)
\;=\;
\hat p_T\bigl(s \mid x,\, \hat{y}(c),\, y^*,\, \theta_* + t_j\bigr),
\]
where equality may be measured by KL divergence (or total variation).
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Remark.}
The precondition \(t_j = a_j\) (persona / target match) is required.
If, for example, we set \(t_j = \text{strict}\) but the judge \(a_j\) has parameters/configuration
\(\theta_{a_j}\) (prompt, weights, etc.) that map to a \emph{lenient} judge,
then the definition is unsatisfiable.

\paragraph{Remark (preference vs.\ trustworthiness).}
In the current literature, ``trustworthiness'' of an AI judge is almost always operationalized as matching human judgments (i.e., tracking human scores).
``Preference'' is the more common term in RLHF settings (which answer is preferred).
The two notions are close once a frame of reference is fixed, but they are not identical:
one can trust a system whose outputs one does not personally prefer, and vice versa.

\section{Practical instantiations}

\begin{definition}[Common practice (\(m_1\))]
Most work treats an AI judge as trustworthy when its scores correlate with human scores on a panel:
\[
\TI_{m_1}(aj) \;=\; \mathrm{Corr}\bigl(\hat s_{aj},\, \hat s_T\bigr).
\]
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Remark (what \(\mathrm{Corr}\) actually matches).}
Correlation is a property of the \emph{joint} distribution of the paired scores \((\hat s_{aj}(i), \hat s_T(i))\).
It is a dependence / association measure, not a pure marginal moment match and not a full distributional match (\(m_0\)).
\begin{itemize}
  \item Pearson is standardized covariance (a joint second-moment quantity after centering and scaling). It can be 1 even when the means differ (by affine rescaling).
  \item Spearman and Kendall match ranks / pairwise orderings of items; they are still weaker than full distributional equality.
\end{itemize}
A high correlation therefore says that the judge ranks items similarly to the target; it does not say that the two score distributions are close, nor that their means match.

The stability term \(\mathrm{std}(\hat s_{aj})\) (used in \(\TI_{2'}\)) is a pure marginal property of the judge alone and need not match the human at all.

\begin{definition}[Valid-and-stable (\(m_2\) / \(\TI_{2'}\))]
A stricter practical criterion combines validity (correlation) with stability (low repeat variance) via a geometric mean:
\[
\TI_{2'}(aj) \;=\; \Bigl(\mathrm{Corr}\bigl(\hat s_{aj},\, \hat s_T\bigr) \cdot \bigl(1 - \mathrm{std}(\hat s_{aj})\bigr)\Bigr)^{1/2}.
\]
This is the working definition of trustworthiness\({}_{2'}\) used in these notes.
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Remark (Sanmi's objection to the stability term).}
The term \(1 - \mathrm{std}(\hat s_{aj})\) is a pure marginal property of the judge.
It does not attempt to match the human's own variability.
Consequently a judge that is artificially more stable (or less stable) than the humans can still score highly, provided the ranking agreement remains strong.
This is a deliberate design choice (penalize unreliable judges) rather than a pure ``match the human'' criterion.
Full distributional match of both location and scale would be more faithful but is currently impractical.
Natural alternatives for later work include:
\begin{itemize}
  \item also matching \(\mathrm{std}(\hat s_{aj}) \approx \mathrm{std}(\hat s_T)\),
  \item using a concordance measure (e.g.\ Lin's CCC) that penalizes both location and scale differences,
  \item or reporting correlation and judge stability as two separate numbers instead of a single geometric mean.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Remark (ideal vs.\ practical).}
The ideal target is full distributional match of scores once a persona is fixed (Definition~\ref{def:tw}).
In practice this is expensive: many humans and many repeated scores would be required.
We therefore approximate the ideal by an association of collapsed scores (correlation) plus a judge-side second moment (low repeat std), combined by geometric mean to obtain \(\TI_{2'}\).
The practical criterion only captures limited aspects of the ideal match---and the std conjunct is not a match to humans at all.
