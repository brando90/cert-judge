\paragraph{Notation note.}
\(\hpT\) denotes the target-judge pdf/pmf; the hat indicates that the estimate is finite-sample.
We write \(\hpT\) (or \(\hat{p}_{H}\) when the target is human) and \(\hpAJ\) for the AI-judge side.
In short: we are comparing a \emph{finite-sample AI judge} against a \emph{finite-sample human} target.

\section{Behaviors and agents}
\label{sec:tw}

\begin{definition}[Universally trustworthy AI judge]
\label{def:tw}
Let \(B\) be a set of benchmarks and \(A\) a set of agents.
An AI judge \(aj \in AJ\) is universally trustworthy if, for every benchmark \(b \in B\) and every agent/solver \(a \in A\),
\(aj\) distributionally matches the corresponding \emph{target judge} (\(tj \in TJ\))
(e.g., a human, a persona, a strict rubric, a lenient rubric, etc.):
\[
\forall b \in B,\; \forall a \in A,\quad
\text{if } t_j = a_j
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\hat{p}_{\mathrm{AJ}}\bigl(s \mid x,\, \hat{y}(c),\, y^*,\, \theta_j\bigr)
\;=\;
\hat{p}_{TJ}\bigl(s \mid x,\, \hat{y}(c),\, y^*,\, \theta_* + t_j\bigr),
\]
where equality is e.g.\ \(\mathrm{KL}=0\).
\end{definition}

\paragraph{Remark.}
The precondition \(t_j = a_j\) (persona / target match) is required.
If, for example, we set \(t_j = \text{strict}\) but the judge \(a_j\) has parameters/configuration
\( e.g., \theta_{a_j}\) (prompt, weights, etc.) that to a \emph{lenient} judge,
then the definition is unsatisfiable.

\paragraph{Remark.}
The literature often uses \emph{preference} as a synonym for human judgment.
The same word is also used for the objective, so preference is the more general term: it includes trustworthiness.
Once a frame of reference is chosen (an objective or not; e.g.\ a persona), there is no leftover contrast of the form ``this is trustworthy but I do not prefer it,'' or the reverse.
