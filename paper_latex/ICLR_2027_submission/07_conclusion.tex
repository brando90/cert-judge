\section{Conclusion}
Agents now write the trust artifacts for their own code, and a language-model judge decides whether those artifacts say the right thing.
We asked whether label-free behavioral properties can take over part of the human validation of that judge, and we tested the answer on judges the properties were not selected on.
One property transfers: the fall in score as theorem statements are deleted from the reference predicts held-out judges' agreement with expert ratings under our pre-registered gates, with a wide interval and an association carried by prompt groups rather than by differences among the judges one would deploy.
The two indices our earlier work proposed do not transfer, choosing a judge by the transferred screen does not beat a fixed human-mirror prompt, a semantic screen passes 15 of 56 configurations, and a score-optimizing attacker inflates the attacked judges and the validated reference alike.
Label-free screens can rule judges out and expose failures that human agreement hides; choosing among the judges one would deploy still needs a human panel, and the decisive test of any replacement is a frozen screen scored against new ratings.
