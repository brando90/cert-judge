\section{Conclusion}
Agents now write the trust artifacts for their own code, and a language-model judge decides whether those artifacts say the right thing.
We replaced the per-judge human validation of that judge with one human panel and four falsifiable properties that fix how scores must respond to controlled edits.
On the validation set, the fixed equal-weight index tracks judge-level human agreement across three families, and the property report exposes a lexical judge that human agreement rates as acceptable.
The report, not the scalar, is the screen's verdict, and a locked test must still establish transfer beyond the labels used to select the protocol.
Human judgment then becomes a reusable foundation: collected once, it anchors automated, falsifiable checks that every new judge must pass.
