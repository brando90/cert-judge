\section*{Reproducibility Statement}
Every number in Sections~\ref{sec:transfer}--\ref{sec:gaming} is read from committed receipts of the four studies behind them: the transfer study's locked protocol, probe manifest, per-configuration harvests, sealed selection and test receipts; the selection-utility study's cached-score analysis; the semantic screen's Lean-validated probe manifest, per-configuration tables, and association receipt; and the gaming study's trajectories and analysis receipt.
The train, validation, and test rows of Table~\ref{tab:transfer} and Figure~\ref{fig:transfer} are recomputed from the committed unit files without model calls, as are the two prompt-group rows, the selection-utility comparison, and the gaming association.
Every judge call went through one hosted gateway with recorded model identifiers, prompt hashes, and per-cell status, and missing cells stay missing in every denominator.
Appendix~\ref{app:judge-prompts} names the judge models and prompt families; Appendix~\ref{app:index-definitions} specifies the components and indices, including numerical edge cases.
The earlier 72-pair validation set and its frozen score vectors reproduce the appendix tables exactly (Appendix~\ref{app:validation-set-72}).
The expert ratings that define the criterion were collected by \textsc{VeriBench} under its own rating protocol and are reused here unchanged.

\section*{Ethics Statement}
The criterion ratings were supplied by expert raters recruited for \textsc{VeriBench}; the earlier validation set, now in the appendix, was rated by an author.
The study evaluates generated Lean code and formal statements, without personal data in the judged artifacts.
It makes no deployment claim and does not establish that screened judges can replace expert review in consequential settings.

\section*{Artificial Intelligence Use Statement}
LLMs generated the candidate Lean artifacts, served as the judges under study, acted as the score-optimizing attacker, and formed the reference ensemble of the gaming study.
Coding-assistant subagents proposed the weakening edits of the semantic screen, which were admitted only through the Lean checks, and helped implement experiments and analysis scripts, check and interpret results, and prepare figures.
They also assisted with methodological feedback, literature retrieval, manuscript drafting, and editing under author supervision.
The human ratings used throughout were supplied by human raters, not generated by these tools.
The authors are responsible for the manuscript's claims, citations, and interpretation of the recorded results.
