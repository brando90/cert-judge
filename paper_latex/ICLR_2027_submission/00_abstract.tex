\begin{abstract}
  % CS197 move 1: problem motivation
  LLM judges are increasingly used to evaluate AI-generated code and formal specifications, especially where passing tests or type-checking cannot show that an artifact captures its intended meaning.
  % CS197 move 2: prior assumption
  Practitioners typically establish a judge's reliability through agreement with human ratings and must repeat that check for every new judge, prompt, model, and domain.
  % CS197 move 3: bit flip / contribution
  We ask whether one human panel can instead be amortized through falsifiable behavioral properties that any new judge is tested on automatically.
  % CS197 move 4: instantiation
  We propose a property-certification protocol for LLM judges of reference-based Lean~4 specification quality: how completely and correctly a candidate specification captures a gold reference.
  Each judge is tested for identity, monotonicity under injected bugs, monotonicity under removed specifications, and repeat/prompt stability.
  A variance-aware trust index, \(\mathrm{TI}_{\mathrm{var}}\), summarizes bug monotonicity and score stability with fixed equal weights.
  % CS197 move 5: evaluation
  Across three judge families with eight configurations each, including weaker GPT-4o-mini judges, a lexical grader, an edit-distance scorer, and a random control, \(\mathrm{TI}_{\mathrm{var}}\) is positively associated with judge-level human agreement on a 72-pair validation set.
  For Codex judges, the Spearman correlation is \(0.833\) on averaged labels and \(0.905\) and \(0.738\) on the two rating passes.
  Specification monotonicity is the most informative single property, and the index flags a lexical judge whose human agreement alone looks acceptable.
  % CS197 move 6: implication (vision closing approved by Brando 09-22; the development-panel caveat stays in move 5 and in Section 6; the VeriBench ranking line was dropped because that table's Claude 3.7 judge is not a certified configuration, Section 5.4)
  Judge validation thus moves from a per-judge annotation cost toward a one-time human panel plus automated, falsifiable checks that any new judge must pass.
\end{abstract}
