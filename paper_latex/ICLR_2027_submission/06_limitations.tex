\section{Limitations and a Locked Test}\label{sec:limitations-test}
\paragraph{Validation evidence, not a test.}
The same labels informed the index form, repeat aggregation, threshold, and prompt panel, so the reported correlations are optimistic validation-set estimates.
They rest on one annotator and eight configurations per family, with shared controls rather than independent samples of eight models.
No test-set result establishes reliability for a new judge or artifact distribution.
We also collected 343 labels from five raters over 78 candidate--gold pairs.
That pool uses statement coverage with a theorem-spam deduction, a different rubric from the panel's holistic ratings.
Its Spearman agreement with the panel labels is approximately $0.13$ on 63 shared pairs.
Two pre-registered screens asked whether property indices tracked agreement with that pool.
The screen of 14 configurations was underpowered; the expanded screen of 32 found no index meeting its registered criterion.
Its largest absolute Spearman association was $-0.39$ with Holm-adjusted permutation $p=0.25$; leave-one-out selection transfer yielded $\rho=0.24$, $p=0.49$.
These negative results do not demonstrate transfer under the original criterion, nor can the pool serve as a test set for that criterion.
Appendix~\ref{app:negative-results} records the corresponding negative-control evidence.

\paragraph{What the screen covers.}\label{sec:limitations-finite}
The probes do not establish sensitivity to vacuity, subtle weakening, over-constraint, or formatting-only edits beyond the four rewrites studied here.
Constructing probes with known directions for these failures is the next methodological step; we claim no result for them.
The historical alpha generator can produce malformed text, so its low scores do not establish sensitivity to valid alpha-renaming.
Other scripted rewrites and the intended ordering of bug edits also lack semantic validation.
The language-model configurations use single-call rubric prompts per repeat, so structured multi-stage judges~\citep{TongZhang2024CodeJudge} are untested.
No automated equivalence baseline on interface-matched pairs was run (Section~\ref{sec:problem-setup}).
We cannot separate rubric ambiguity from misunderstanding Lean semantics with the current annotations.
Presentation, including redundant theorems, implementation bloat, and unnecessary abstraction, is unassessed; holistic human ratings may reflect it implicitly.
We did not optimize candidates against the judge, so resistance to gaming is untested.

\paragraph{Scope.}
All candidates and probes come from \textsc{VeriBench} in Lean~4.
The protocol could be instantiated for another proof assistant or for AI-written tests, but neither cross-assistant nor cross-benchmark transfer has been tested.
A score is relative to the supplied gold reference and extraction rule, not a guarantee that all intended requirements are present.
Reference-free screening and the number of annotators needed for a stable criterion remain open.
The index also depends on its comparison panel and omits constant ladders with undefined correlations; these implementation choices limit portable screening.

\paragraph{A locked test.}
A decisive test would first freeze the rubric, property generators, score extraction, aggregation, index, normalization reference, and any rejection rule.
Independent raters would then label new candidate--gold pairs under that rubric, with new judge configurations fixed before their labels are examined.
The analysis would report both artifact-level agreement and judge-level association, including uncertainty and property-specific failures.
A separate distribution-shift test would be needed for new domains.
Because the original rubric text is unavailable, recovering it or validating an explicitly reconstructed criterion is a prerequisite to claiming a matched-rubric test.
