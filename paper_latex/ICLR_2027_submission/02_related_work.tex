\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Validating LLM judges through human agreement.}
\textsc{G-Eval}~\citep{Liu2023GEval} and \textsc{MT-Bench}~\citep{Zheng2023MTBench} establish a judge's reliability by its agreement with human ratings, so each new judge or task calls for the same comparison.
\citet{Kocmi2024HumanVsLLM} measure that agreement across 20 tasks and find it task dependent, which is why the comparison cannot be made once for all judges.
\citet{Li2024TrustLLMJudgments} measure consistency across repeated judgments, which detects instability but not a consistently wrong judge.
We keep human agreement as the criterion, collect it once, and ask whether checks whose required direction is known without ratings predict it for judges they were not selected on.

\paragraph{AI judges for AI-written trust artifacts.}
\textsc{ICE-Score}~\citep{Zhuo2024ICEScore} and \textsc{CodeJudge}~\citep{TongZhang2024CodeJudge} score generated code with a language model where execution gives incomplete evidence, and validate the judge by its agreement with human or execution-based labels on their own tasks.
\textsc{SWT-Bench} validates agent-written tests by execution against the developers' fix~\citep{mundler2024swtbench}, which requires the test and the reference to share one interface, the condition that end-to-end formalization breaks.
For formal statements, \textsc{FormalAlign} learns an alignment score between informal and formal statements~\citep{lu2024formalalign}, whereas our target is the behavior of a judge comparing candidate and gold specifications.
Prover pipelines filter autoformalized statements with an LLM judge~\citep{lin2025goedelprover,wang2025kiminaprover}.
\textsc{ReForm} validates its statement judge against expert annotations~\citep{chen2026reform}, evidence that does not transfer to the next judge configuration.
\textsc{VeriBench} scores agent-written Lean specifications with an LLM judge~\citep{miranda2026veribench}; we study when such judges can be trusted.
Each of these judges answers a question that execution or proof checking cannot settle: whether an artifact means the right thing.

\paragraph{Behavioral tests with a known direction.}
Metamorphic testing checks a program without an oracle by fixing how its output must change under a transformation of its input~\citep{chen1998metamorphic}.
The test-oracle problem it addresses~\citep{barr2015oracle} is the one an AI-written trust artifact raises: running the artifact cannot tell whether the artifact is right.
\textsc{CheckList} applies the same idea to NLP models with templated invariance and directional tests, and reveals failures in models with high held-out accuracy~\citep{ribeiro2020checklist}.
Our properties are metamorphic relations on the judge: identity, ladder monotonicity, and rewrite invariance fix the required response to a scripted edit of a gold artifact.
The one-time human panel adds what metamorphic testing alone lacks, a measurement of whether the relations track the human criterion across judges, and the held-out judge split measures whether that association survives on judges the relations were not chosen on.
