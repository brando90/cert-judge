\section{Property Certification Protocol}\label{sec:property-protocol}
\paragraph{One-time human panel.}
Humans label a fixed validation set once; in our study, one annotator rated candidate--gold pairs under two rubric strictness levels (Section~\ref{sec:data-human-protocol}).
For each judge configuration, we then compute property scores on scripted perturbations of Lean artifacts, which requires judge calls but no human labels.
The panel is used only to measure how strongly property scores track human agreement (Table~\ref{tab:protocol_workflow}).

\paragraph{Properties.}
A trustworthy judge should satisfy four falsifiable behavioral targets.
\textbf{P1 (identity):} an artifact compared with itself receives the maximum score, \(J(x,x)=1\).
\textbf{P2 (bug monotonicity):} scores do not increase as semantic bugs are introduced cumulatively.
\textbf{P3 (specification monotonicity):} scores do not increase as theorem statements and unit tests are removed; we keep this historical name although, by the definition in Section~\ref{sec:problem-setup}, removed tests are not specification.
\textbf{P4 (stability):} scores stay stable across repeated calls and under semantics-preserving rewrites such as \textsc{Alpha}-renaming, which renames bound variables and binders without changing what the artifact means.
We measure monotonicity with Kendall's $\tau$, Spearman's $\rho$, and pairwise ordering accuracy, following prior judge-evaluation work~\citep{TongZhang2024CodeJudge,Zhuo2024ICEScore}.
Because each ladder level extends the previous one, later levels are more damaged by construction, and the expected ordering does not depend on how severe any single edit is.

\paragraph{Trust indices.}
The property scores \(\text{ident}\) (P1), \(\text{mono}\) (P2), \(\text{weak}\) (P3), and \(\text{stab}\) (P4) lie in $[0,1]$ and are graded targets, not binary gates.
As baselines, the arithmetic indices \(v1\) and \(v2\) take fixed weighted averages of them, and GM aggregates monotonicity and stability geometrically (Appendix~\ref{app:index-definitions}).
Our primary index, \(\mathrm{TI}_{\mathrm{var}}\), is the geometric mean of a bug-monotonicity term and two stability terms, with fixed equal weights:
\[
  \begin{aligned}
    \mathrm{TI}_{\mathrm{var}}
    ={} & \exp\!\Big(
    \tfrac{1}{3}\log C_f
    + \tfrac{1}{3}\log(1-\widetilde{\mathrm{VarCorr}}) \\
        & \qquad\quad
    + \tfrac{1}{3}\log(1-\widetilde{\mathrm{VarScore}})
    \Big),
  \end{aligned}
\]
where \(C_f\) is the mean per-file bug-ladder monotonicity term and \(\widetilde{\mathrm{VarCorr}},\widetilde{\mathrm{VarScore}}\) are normalized variance penalties (Appendix~\ref{app:index-definitions}).
Because the index is multiplicative, one weak term pulls it down, and the variance penalties favor judges that behave consistently across repeats and prompts.
Our hypothesis is that judges with higher \(\mathrm{TI}_{\mathrm{var}}\) agree more with humans on the validation set.
