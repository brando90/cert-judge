## Why the old 0.6 / 0.8 / 0.8 (and 0.833 / 0.905 / 0.738) looked odd
- **Where the numbers are.** Cert-judge paper panel: Codex 0.833, Claude Opus 0.905, Qwen3-Coder 0.738 judge-human Spearman (reproduced to 3 decimals in experiment 41, G0). trustworthy-theorem-judges experiment 22: the selected "0.6, 0.8, 0.8" VeriBench judge "jumped across train / validation / test (Spearman = 0.6400, 0.8800, 0.8257)".
- **Eight judges per family.** The paper's headline is a Spearman over 8 judge series: one rank swap moves it by about 0.05 to 0.1; exact permutation p = 0.015 for Codex, with an interval of [0.238, 0.976] (experiment 41).
- **One annotator, old prompt.** The paper labels are one annotator's two old-prompt passes. On the same 59 artifacts they have Spearman -0.04 with the corrected VeriBench panel (T3 below), so a high correlation with them says little about the corrected rubric.
- **Few distinct judge scores.** The headline series take only 8 (Codex), 5 (Opus) and 4 (Qwen) distinct values over the 59 artifacts, so ties dominate rank correlations.
- **Few tasks.** 20 tasks: a random 4-task subset gives a Spearman spread of 1.0 to 1.4 in width, and the 20-task bootstrap width is 0.5 to 0.8 (T5).
- **Reference bytes.** Experiment 41 found that for only 7 of the 24 scored tasks the reference file the judge saw matches what the rater saw; part of the negative numbers on corrected labels may be this mismatch, not the judge. I did not re-test it here.

## Do more data remove the problem? (Brando's question)
- **Old alone** (59 artifacts, 20 tasks, 4.5 ratings): interval width of the headline Spearman 0.53 to 0.80; human ceiling about 0.84.
- **New alone** (100 files, 25 tasks, 2.0 to 2.5 ratings): no judge scores yet, so no judge interval; the judge-free edit-distance baseline has width 0.46 (vs 0.70 on old) and the two complete raters agree at Spearman 0.62, within 1 point on 100% of files, weighted kappa 0.75; 73% of ratings are 5, so there is little spread to rank (a ceiling effect that caps any correlation).
- **Pooled** (45 tasks, 159 artifacts): the baseline interval width falls to 0.43 with within-set ranks; the raw pooled correlation +0.50 is a **set-mixing artifact** (old items have mean rating 3.05 and edit similarity 0.06, new ones 4.57 and 0.23: a between-set difference, while within each set the sign is negative). Pool only after ranking within each set or stratifying, and judge scores on the new files are needed to do it for the judges.
- **Learning curve** (T5, T6): spread falls from about 1.0 at 4 to 10 tasks to about 0.3 at 30 tasks and 0.16 at 40 of 45; the point estimate stays near -0.2 to -0.35 for the judges at every size: the estimate was already stable, the interval was the weak part.
- **What more data cannot fix:** a judge whose score does not carry information about the human distribution stays at the constant forecast however many tasks arrive, and a correlation hides leniency and scale mismatches (affine invariance), which RPS, CE and TVD do not. That is the reason to report RPS first.

## Where this project and Corr vs CE disagree
- Corr vs CE layer 4: CE-fitted calibration had lower held-out RPS than Pearson-fitted in all 35 judge-by-budget cells (purl.stanford.edu/bh388th6008). **Here: 13 of 24 series (point estimates, mean difference +0.0001 RPS in favour of Pearson-fit), 15 of 24 on CE, 0 of 24 on TVD (Pearson-fit better on TVD in all 24).** No cell is significantly separated. Likely reason: those judges carry almost no signal about the corrected labels (calibrated RPS about equal to the constant), so any fit collapses to the marginal; layer 4 separates the two fits only when the judge has signal. This agrees with the direction of the paper (Corr does not tell you the judge is useful) but not with its size here.
- The Trust Index: TI_var is **negatively** associated with all alignments on corrected labels (T7: -0.10 to -0.74; Qwen TVD p = 0.046, the rest p between 0.06 and 0.84, 12 comparisons, no correction); higher TI means no better and nominally worse RPS, CE and TVD. Eight judges per family: indicative only.

## Failed or only partly true pre-registered predictions
- P1 (raw judge scores worse than constant on RPS): true for all 24 series (best raw 0.193 vs constant 0.174).
- P2 (CE-fit lowers RPS vs Pearson-fit in a majority): met by the frozen criterion (13 of 24), but the mean difference is +0.0001 RPS, so it is **not a result**; CE-fit vs constant: interval includes 0 for all 24 series (true).
- P3 (half-width above 0.2 at 20 tasks): true (0.26 to 0.40); the 1/sqrt(k) scaling is not established (see Deviations 3).
- P4 (over 60% at 5: true, 73%; "agree only moderately": Spearman 0.62 and kappa 0.75 are moderate to good; "reliability lower than H_vb": **false**, two-rater Spearman-Brown 0.77 vs 0.70 on old).
- P5 (TI does not track proper-score alignment): **challenged** descriptively: the associations are negative (higher TI, worse alignment; Qwen p = 0.046 to 0.058), with no frozen criterion or multiplicity rule, so no broad rejection is claimed.

## Deviations from PREREG (dated addendum 11-24-2026, after the run)
1. **Population count:** PREREG says H_vb has 60 items; the rule (at least 4 ratings) gives **59** (one item, `13_MyUppercase__agent1.lean`, has three ratings). The rule was followed; the count in PREREG is corrected here.
2. **Draws:** all intervals use 2,000 task-bootstrap draws and seed 0 **except T7 (TI versus alignment), which used 500**.

4. **Exploratory, not pre-registered:** pooled old plus new tables, the within-set-rank pooling and its learning curve (T4, T6), added after seeing that raw pooling gave a spurious +0.50.
5. **CE-fit definition:** maximum likelihood plus a small ridge (1e-3 x beta squared) and predicted category probabilities clipped at 0.001 then renormalised; these two details were not frozen.
6. **Baseline orientation:** PREREG says normalized edit distance; the code uses normalized similarity (the experiment 41 / paper control definition), so the sign of its Spearman is the reverse of a distance's.
7. **Reporting gaps:** the planned H_paper secondary panel, the CE/TVD/Pearson intervals, the leave-one-task-out range of the headline Spearman and the random-score baseline are in `results/*.csv` and `results/*_summary.json` but not in the tables above.
