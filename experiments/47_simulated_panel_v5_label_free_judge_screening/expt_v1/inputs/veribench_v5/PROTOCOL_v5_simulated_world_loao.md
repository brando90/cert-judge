## What this condition can and cannot establish

Every Q1 and Q2 number is agreement with a **simulated panel**, not with experts. Stage S1 measures, before any interpretation, how far each panel member and each panel are from the experts on the 75 rated items. For scale: stage 1 measured Gemini 3.1 Pro high at Spearman 0.624 [0.293, 0.835] (personas) and 0.626 [0.296, 0.827] (instructions only) against the expert mean; the experts agree with each other at 0.879; the frozen judge agrees with the experts at 0.76 on all 75 items (descriptive, it was built on them) and 0.834 [0.553, 0.939] on its 45 held-out items; and the judge agreed with the Gemini panel (ICC(A,1) 0.875) more than either agreed with the experts. A panel less accurate than the judge cannot show that the judge is **right** where they agree; it can show **where** judge–panel agreement changes with the agent. A diverse panel may be more accurate than any single member, and S1 measures whether it is; the interpretation rule in § Gates ties the reading of Q1 and Q2 to that measured accuracy.

Q3 is the human-grounded result, with one stated condition: the recipe's prompt family (H2) and its two models were selected in experiment 100 using these 75 items' expert ratings, so Q3 measures whether conventions and rated examples rebuilt **without** human ratings recover the human-built recipe's agreement, conditional on that human-selected recipe (experiment 104's comparator carries the same condition).

**Licensed wording.** "Against a simulated panel of [k] frontier model families each playing our three expert raters (panel–expert agreement Spearman [S1 value] on 75 items), the frozen FSC judge [and the judge-building recipe rebuilt without the held-out agent] agreed with the panel on [n] held-out agent groups at …; same-family effects were [B1–B3 values]; with conventions and rated examples rebuilt from simulated ratings only, the recipe's judge agreed with the expert ratings on the one human-rated agent at … ." **Forbidden on this evidence:** "the judge generalizes to unseen agents", "validated on unseen agents", "trustworthy on any agent", "built with no human input". Experiment 102's human rating round carries the generalization claims.

## Design decisions (each with its reason)

1. **A panel of flagship models from seven families, one model per family, equal family weight** (§ Panel). Brando's decision: diversity of model families, rather than avoiding the judge's own families, is how same-family bias is reduced and exposed.
2. **Every model plays the three expert raters as personas.** P1, P2 and P3 are the three expert raters (P1 and P2 rated all 75 items, P3 rated 45); the expert target used throughout experiments 100–106 is their mean. Each persona prompt is stage 1's construction, unchanged: the locked frame text, the raters' instructions verbatim, and *k* = 6 of that rater's own earlier ratings on other tasks (band rule, round-robin low/high/mid order, seed SHA-256(item id + persona + "106") mod 2^32). P4 and P5 are not personas: they are the non-expert raters, and the panel stands in for the expert target.
3. **Every model also rates with the instructions-only frame (C0)**, stage 1's control: the same frame without persona or examples. This defines a second simulated world built from no human rating, and measures across seven families whether the personas change the ratings at all (stage 1 found they did not for Gemini).
4. **Two simulated worlds, both fully cross-validated.** The **persona world** (target T_all) is Brando's panel and is primary for Q1, Q2 and Q5. The **instructions-only world** (target T_C0) uses no human rating anywhere in its ratings, so it is the world in which the recipe can be checked against the experts on the 75 items without leakage (Q3) and the world whose deployable judge Q4 can evaluate. A judge built in the persona world has seen the 75 items' expert ratings indirectly (they are the persona examples), so its human-anchor numbers are reported as secondary with that leakage stated.
5. **Same-family raters are included on purpose and their bias is measured** (Q5) instead of avoided. The judge's members are GPT-6 Sol and Claude Opus 5.5; the OpenAI and Anthropic raters are the families' flagships (GPT-6 Astra, Claude Fable 5.1), not the judge's own models.
6. **S1 accuracy never selects or reweights raters.** Choosing raters by their agreement with the experts on the 75 items would leak those items into Q3. A model is excluded only if its live controls fail or if 20% or more of its S1 or S2 cells end in a route failure (PROVIDER_ERROR, INFRA_ERROR, MISSING); the route-failure share is evaluated from receipt categories alone, per model, before any agreement statistic is read, and every exclusion is recorded with its reason.
7. **Fresh tasks outside the human rating round.** Experiment 102's round tasks (sets A, B and N) are excluded, so nothing here touches a sealed item or shows Brando (rater A1) a judge value on a round item. The 25 originally rated tasks are excluded from the fresh draw, so the G00 fold is task-disjoint from its build set.
8. **50 tasks, 10 per split.** Task-bootstrap intervals are driven by the number of tasks; 25 gave intervals of about ±0.25 in experiments 100 and 104. Valkyrie capacity is not a constraint (Brando, 09-28-2026).
9. **Every judge is re-run fresh, through one route, in one window.** Frozen-H2 ratings already exist for many items (experiments 102, 103), partly from the retired host-side gateway route. All judges run through Valkyrie custom agents; existing ratings serve only as a reproduction check.
10. **The procedure under test is experiment 104's automated recipe, applied through a versioned adapter** (§ Recipe adapter): at most four conventions written by Claude Opus 5.5 max from the nine largest disagreements between the instructions-only GPT-6 Sol member and the world's target on the build set, rated examples by experiment 100's quantile rule, experiment 100's H2 prompt family, GPT-6 Sol high plus Claude Opus 5.5 max. The prompt family and the two models are not re-searched; the claim is about the recipe's learned parts. Differences from experiment 104, all disclosed: build sets are agent groups, targets are simulated and fractional, and each rebuilt-judge member uses 3 calls (104 used 5).
11. **Shared tasks, leave-task-out examples.** All groups answer the same 50 tasks, as in deployment, where a judge meets new agents on known benchmark tasks. A held-out item's rated examples never come from its own task, and conventions may not name any task (experiment 104's forbidden-identifier rule over all 75 task names of this condition).
12. **Blank outputs** (empty or whitespace-only files, or recorded void placeholders such as `-- LLM call failed`) get rating 0 from every rater and judge without a call. They are excluded from the primary statistics (they agree trivially) and included in a declared inclusive sensitivity.
13. **Repeats.** S1: 3 calls per (model, frame, item), as stage 1. S2: 1 call per (model, frame, item); the panel average over many slots carries the noise reduction; a seeded 20% audit subset of S2 items gets 2 more calls in every slot to measure repeat SD. Judges: 3 calls per member.
14. **Parser.** For every rater, parser v2 is primary (decision D1's condition held in stage 1: 0 cells changed score); the locked `Score: N` parser is a sensitivity. Judge and rule-writer outputs use experiment 100's and 104's parsers unchanged.
15. **Missing data never produces a pass** (§ Aggregation; § Gates): coverage minimums are frozen, comparisons use identical paired items, and an under-covered or uncomputable statistic yields INCONCLUSIVE.
16. **Every call's timeout chain is frozen and verified** (§ Identity checks 6), so no valid long call is cut short by an outer limit.

## Aggregation and simulated targets (all on 0–1: the 0–5 rating divided by 5)

- **Rater slot value.** S2: the slot's first call, COMPLETE under parser v2. S1: the median of the slot's COMPLETE calls, defined only if at least 2 of its 3 calls are COMPLETE. Audit repeats measure repeat SD only.
- **Family value in the persona world.** The mean of the family's available persona slots; defined only if at least 2 of its persona slots are available (on G00 items without a P3 rating, both P1 and P2 are required).
- **T_all (persona world, primary).** The mean of the available family values: every family weighs the same, whatever its number of slots. Defined only if at least two thirds of the panel's families (rounded up) have a value; otherwise the item's target is missing.
- **T_C0 (instructions-only world).** The mean over families of each family's C0 slot, with the same two-thirds rule.
- **T_xjudge:** T_all over families other than OpenAI and Anthropic (the judge members' families), defined if at least 3 such families have a value. **T_xagent:** per item, T_all over families other than the item's agent family (nothing removed for G03, whose Mistral family has no rater), with the two-thirds rule applied to the remaining families. **Per-model targets:** each model's own family value, for robustness.
- **Judge member value.** The median of the member's COMPLETE calls, defined only if at least 2 of 3 are COMPLETE. **Judge arm value** (F, Z, L): the mean of the two members' values, defined only if both are defined; there is no single-member fallback.
- **Coverage.** For each (group, arm, world), coverage = items with both the judge value and the target defined, divided by the group's non-blank items. Paired comparisons (L − F, L − Z, every Q3 comparison) use only the items on which both compared ratings and the target are defined.

## Recipe adapter (versioned code in `scripts/v5_recipe_adapter.py`, built and tested before the lock)

Experiment 104's code assumes experiment 100's 75 human-rated items: its renderer loads them internally and filters by task, its demonstration formatter prints integer `p1/p2/p3` ratings, and its error selection reads experiment 100's own instructions-only runs. The adapter re-implements those three inputs and leaves every template string of experiments 100 and 104 untouched:

- **Explicit build items.** Every fold build takes an explicit list of build item ids (the other groups' non-blank items with a defined target and Z value) and the world's target for each; nothing is loaded from experiment 100's labels. The held-out group's items can never enter a build pool or an example pool.
- **Base predictions (experiment 104's rule, exactly).** For each build item, the instructions-only **GPT-6 Sol member** of arm Z: the median of its COMPLETE calls and the rationale of the call closest to that median (ties by repeat index).
- **Error cases.** The nine build items with the largest |Sol median / 5 − target|, ties broken by item id (experiment 104's rule); experiment 104's `RULE_SYSTEM`, `RULE_USER_HEADER` and `CASE_TEMPLATE` render them unchanged.
- **Rated examples.** Experiment 100's quantile rule (`DEMO_QUANTILES` 0.1, 0.4, 0.7, 0.95; one item per task; closest to each quantile of the targets, ties by item id) on the build items whose task differs from the scored item's task; experiment 100's `DEMO_HEADER` and `DEMO_TEMPLATE` render them unchanged.
- **Simulated scores in the templates.** Where the templates print the experts' scores, the adapter prints the world's raters with one decimal and never truncates: in the persona world `P1=x.x, P2=x.x, P3=x.x` (each persona's family-balanced mean, P3 omitted where it has no slot, as the human format omits a missing rater), in the instructions-only world `C0=x.x`; the mean fields print the target exactly as experiment 104 prints the expert mean. All other bytes are experiment 100's and 104's.
- **Forbidden identifiers and parsing.** Experiment 104's parser and forbidden-identifier rule, with the identifier list built from all 75 task names of this condition. A rule-writer cell whose output fails that parser gets one declared re-send (counted); if the re-send also fails, that fold's L arm is INCONCLUSIVE.
- **Required offline tests (before the lock).** (1) With experiment 104's own inputs (human targets, task folds, experiment 100's H0 runs), the adapter reproduces 104's recorded rule-writer prompt hashes and rebuilt-judge prompt hashes byte for byte. (2) No held-out item id appears in any build pool, error list or example list of its fold, in either world. (3) Fractional targets survive into the rendered text (a target of 3.67/5 prints as 3.7, never 3). (4) Changing any human rating in experiment 100's `items.jsonl` leaves every instructions-only-world fold prompt byte-identical, and every persona-world fold prompt byte-identical given fixed simulated ratings. (5) Two renders of every fold are byte-identical.

## Folds

Per world (persona world with T_all, instructions-only world with T_C0), one fold per group G00–G13 (14 folds). For held-out group *g*:

- **Build set** = the non-blank items of the other 13 groups (fresh-task groups and G00) with a defined target and Z value. For *g* = G00 the build set is G01–G13 only, so the G00 fold has seen no rated task.
- **Conventions, rated examples and prompt** = the recipe through the adapter, with that world's target.
- **Held-out ratings** = arm L for every non-blank item of *g*.

**The all-groups judge, per world:** the same construction with build set G00–G13. It is the judge the simulated procedure would deploy; the instructions-only world's all-groups judge is Q4's candidate. Both all-groups judges are built, their conventions, examples and prompt hashes committed, before the main analysis ends; they rate only the late groups.

## Identity checks and controls before any manifest call (separate task ids, never manifest cells)

1. **Rater prompts:** for 3 G00 items per frame, the rendered system and user bytes equal stage 1's recorded prompt hashes (offline), and are identical across all panel models (only the model selector differs).
2. **Rater transport, per model:** the request `vb106_rater` (or a versioned successor, if a provider needs a different request envelope) sends for that model is recorded and checked against the gateway's registry entry for the selector (no fallback models, only allowed provider-config keys, the frozen reasoning setting, temperature and output limit). For Gemini 3.1 Pro, the delivered request must be byte-identical to stage 1's for its stage-1 cells to count as panel cells if it ever replaces Argon. Then 2 live control calls per model on one ordinary battery task: oracle (expect ≥ 4 of 5) and blank (expect ≤ 1). A model that fails is excluded before admission (decision 6).
2b. **Gemini 4 Argon smoke test (Brando, 10-05-2026), before anything else is admitted:** record the registry entry of `google/gemini-4-argon`; send one oracle and one blank control call through the panel's rater agent (separate task ids); record status, latency, the parsed rating, the delivered request fields and the reported charge in `REPORT.md` and the checkpoint, and commit them at once. Pass = both calls COMPLETE with oracle ≥ 4 and blank ≤ 1. A fail reinstates Gemini 3.1 Pro (§ Panel).
3. **H2 and H0 judges through `vb103_judge`:** rendered prompts byte-identical to experiment 100's recorded prompt hashes for 3 items per configuration (offline); then per member and configuration, oracle and blank control calls with the same expectations.
4. **Rule writer:** the outgoing request (model, effort, max tokens, prompt bytes) equals experiment 104's rule writer for 104's repetition-1 fold-1 inputs (prompt bytes compared offline through the adapter's test 1; request fields compared with `configs/rule_writer_opus55_max_128k.json`). `vb103_judge` exposes no timeout argument and defaults to a 1,800-second client timeout, below that configuration's 3,600 seconds, so the rule writer runs in a versioned custom agent `vb106_rulewriter` (built from `vb103_judge`, with the client timeout as a contract argument), pushed, downloaded, byte-compared and recorded. One live call on 104's repetition-1 fold-1 input must parse into at most four conventions.
5. **Adapter and renderer:** the five offline tests of § Recipe adapter pass.
6. **Timeout chain:** for every arm, the benchmark-service per-task limit and any Valkyrie task limit are at least twice the agent's client timeout plus 600 seconds (one declared re-send plus receipt writing): raters 2,400-second client, at least 5,400 seconds; judges 1,800-second client, at least 4,200 seconds; rule writer 3,600-second client, at least 7,800 seconds. The worker reads the deployed values back from each service and contract and records them in the manifest before admission.

A failed check stops admission of the affected arm or model; it is diagnosed and recorded, never worked around by changing a locked parameter.

## Statistics (experiment 100's `metrics.py`, unchanged)

Task-cluster bootstrap, 10,000 draws, seed 20261005: tasks are resampled and carry all their items across groups and raters. Primary statistics use non-blank items with defined values (§ Aggregation).

- **S1 (Q0):** for each (model, frame) and for the panels T_all, T_C0 and T_xjudge, against the expert mean (P1–P3, experiment 100's target) and against the P1–P2 mean, on all 75 items and the 60 non-placeholder items: Spearman, Kendall τ-b, Pearson, ICC(A,1), ICC(C,1), MAE, mean bias (rater minus expert, positive = lenient, with both means), repeat SD; the persona labelling rule of PROTOCOL.md per model; persona minus C0 per model (paired); the best single model versus each panel (paired).
- **Per held-out group, per world, for each judge (F, Z, L) against the world's target:** Spearman, Kendall τ-b, Pearson, ICC(A,1) and ICC(C,1), MAE, mean bias (judge minus target, positive = lenient, with both means), target SD, within-group-centered agreement, n items, n tasks, n blank, n missing by category, coverage.
- **Pooled over primary groups** (G01–G13 that qualify; G00 is reported on its own), and **system level:** group mean rating per judge against the group mean target over primary groups: Spearman and Kendall over groups, MAE of group means.
- **Paired differences** (paired task bootstrap on identical items): L − F and L − Z on Spearman, ICC(A,1) and MAE, per group and pooled, per world.
- **Q3:** the instructions-only world's G00-fold judge (primary), the persona world's G00-fold judge (secondary, leakage stated), F, Z and experiment 104's out-of-fold repetition-1 ratings (`results/per_item_oof.csv`) against the expert mean on all 75 items and on the 60 non-placeholder items, and against the P1–P2 mean; each G00-fold judge also against its own world's target on G00, so the simulation's estimate for this fold sits beside the human one.
- **p-values:** every gated statistic gets a one-sided task-bootstrap p-value against its pre-declared threshold (share of bootstrap draws on the failing side), named "task-bootstrap test against threshold"; B1–B3 get two-sided task-bootstrap p-values against zero (twice the smaller tail share).
- **Sensitivities (declared, not gated):** Q1 and Q2 against T_xjudge, T_xagent and each per-model target; inclusive of blanks; the locked parser for raters; audit-subset medians instead of first calls.

## Bias analyses (Q5; persona world; non-blank items of primary groups G01–G13 with defined values)

- **B1, judge–rater family affinity, one effect per judge member *j*** (GPT-6 Sol, OpenAI; Claude Opus 5.5, Anthropic). With *J_j* the member's value from arm F and *R_m* panel model *m*'s family value: affinity_*j* = mean over items of [ mean over other-family models *m* of |*J_j* − *R_m*| − |*J_j* − *R_m\**| ], where *m\** is the panel model of *j*'s own family. Positive = the member is closer to its own family's rater. Not estimable if *m\** is excluded.
- **B2, rater self-preference, one effect per panel model *m* whose family has at least one primary agent group:** with T_all^(−m) the persona target recomputed without *m*'s family, self_pref_*m* = mean over items from *m*'s family's agent groups of (*R_m* − T_all^(−m)) minus the same mean over items from all other groups. Positive = *m* rates its own family's agents more leniently than the rest of the panel does. Families without a primary agent group (xAI, and the Chinese families other than Alibaba) are **not estimable** and reported as such; Alibaba is estimable only if G12 is primary.
- **B3, judge self-preference, one effect for arm F (L reported descriptively):** with b = F − T_xjudge, self_pref_judge = mean of b over items from OpenAI and Anthropic agent groups minus mean of b over items from the other primary groups (G03, G13, and G12 if primary). Positive = the judge is more lenient on its own families' agents.
- **Detection.** The trigger family is {B1 for each estimable member, B3, B2 for each estimable model}. An effect is **detected** when its Holm-adjusted two-sided p-value across that family is below 0.05 and its magnitude is at least 0.05 on the 0–1 scale; unadjusted 95% intervals are reported beside it.
- **Correction.** If B1 or B3 is detected, every Q1 and Q2 verdict is computed again against T_xjudge and reported co-primary; if B2 is detected for any model, against T_xagent as well. A favourable verdict requires every triggered co-primary analysis to pass too.

## Gates, interpretation and verdicts

Thresholds come from experiment 102's C5 and C7 gates, decision D5's non-inferiority margin and experiment 100's gates. Each gate's outcome is PASS, FAIL or INCONCLUSIVE; INCONCLUSIVE means coverage below 90% or a required statistic that cannot be computed (for example an undefined ICC), and is never read as a pass.

- **Per-group gate (each primary group with ≥ 25 non-blank items, per judge arm, per world):** MAE ≤ 0.15 and |bias| ≤ 0.10, plus ICC(A,1) ≥ 0.40 when that group's target SD ≥ 0.10.
- **Pooled gate (per judge arm, per world):** Spearman 95% lower bound > 0.20 and ICC(A,1) ≥ 0.40, on pooled coverage ≥ 90%.
- **Q1 (arm F) and Q2 (arm L), per world:** PASS if the pooled gate passes and every primary group's gate passes; FAIL if the pooled gate fails or any primary group's gate fails; otherwise INCONCLUSIVE. Failing and inconclusive groups are named. Q2 additionally reports non-inferiority to F (pooled paired difference L − F in Spearman and in ICC(A,1), each with 95% lower bound > −0.10) and "the learned parts add agreement on unseen agents" only if pooled L − Z Spearman has 95% lower bound > 0, otherwise "not separable from zero". The persona world is primary for Q1 and Q2.
- **Q3, human anchor.** The instructions-only world's G00-fold judge against the expert mean on 75 items: "non-inferior to the human-built recipe" if the paired Spearman difference (that judge − experiment 104's out-of-fold ratings, identical items) has 95% lower bound > −0.10 and the judge's ICC(A,1) ≥ 0.40, with coverage ≥ 90%; otherwise FAIL or INCONCLUSIVE. Disclosed difference: 3 calls per member here, 5 in experiment 104.
- **Q4, preference.** The instructions-only world's all-groups judge is "preferred on this evidence" only if, in that world, Q2 passes (pooled gate, every primary group's gate, and non-inferiority to F), every triggered co-primary analysis passes, and Q3 passes. The persona world's all-groups judge cannot be checked against humans here without leakage; it is reported, and its human check belongs to experiment 102's round. This experiment deploys nothing: replacing the frozen H2 judge is Brando's decision and would be a new, separately validated condition.
- **Q5.** Every effect is reported by name, size, interval and adjusted p-value, or as not estimable.
- **Interpretation rule (from S1).** If a world's target has Spearman below 0.76 with the expert mean on all 75 items (the frozen judge's own agreement on the same items), that world's Q1 and Q2 are labelled "agreement with a panel less accurate than the judge" in every summary; otherwise "agreement with a panel at least as accurate as the judge on the rated items".

## Completion, failures, durable execution and spend
