## Decision, 10-18-2026: do not lower the gate, and do not fit another screen on this cache

Recorded after experiments 48, 49 and 50 landed on main, before any rater is recruited for this pool. It does not launch the pool, move the 0.45 gate, or retire the pre-registered screen test below.

**Keep the 0.45 gate.** A family-specific lower gate is the wrong repair. On fuller-context prompts alone, the frozen screen's Spearman with human agreement is 0.302 (32 configurations, all judge splits; `experiments/archive/49_human_rating_round_design_precision_and_power_from_cached_judge_scores_and_five_raters/RESULTS_rating_round_design.md`, Q2). The parametric power of the pre-registered test (Spearman ≥ 0.45 and one-sided permutation p-val < 0.05) at a true correlation of 0.30 is 0.23 at 16 judges and 0.09 at 48 (2,000 draws): as the judge count grows, the estimate concentrates below the gate and power falls. Lowering the gate to sit under 0.30 would be a threshold chosen from the cache that already missed it.

**The pooled pass is a between-family contrast.** Experiment 48 permutes the held-out criterion inside groups (16 configurations, 10,000 permutations, seed 48): within judge model p-val=0.0701, within prompt family p-val=0.9136. Resampling the four held-out judge models puts the Spearman of 0.558 in [−0.842, 0.656] (`experiments/archive/48_c_ms_gold_heldout_judge_association_cluster_bootstrap_and_criterion_sensitivity/RESULTS_heldout_association_sensitivity.md`). That is the scope statement, not a new verdict: experiment 39's registered PASS stands.

**Do not fit another screen on these scores.** Experiment `experiments/archive/56_semantic_margin_training_only_diagnosis_and_revisions` (10-19-2026, training rows only) found the same boundary from the other direction: the formatting penalty is 0 on 59/59 task values and the identity baseline is 1, so both frozen margin revisions equal the original margin, and that margin's Spearman with current-five training agreement is −0.378 across 12 correlated setups (p-val=n/a). Repeat total variation on those rows spans 0.111 to 0.267 (Spearman 0.210 with agreement, p-val=n/a). Experiment 50's semantic-margin selector did not beat the fixed human-mirror rubric H2 (expected Spearman 0.815 [0.414, 0.950] versus 0.826 [0.507, 0.934]; paired difference −0.012 [−0.197, 0.183], p-val=n/a, 29 of 30 files, 10 tasks; `experiments/archive/50_semantic_effect_margin_cached_judge_selection_train_val_test/RESULTS_semantic_margin.md`). Another join of the existing caches would be a further look at the same judges. H2 stays the comparison this pool reports selection loss against (O3). O3's threshold stays the one frozen from experiment 39's train and validation units, and a failure of O1 or O3 is a result.

**What the ratings still buy.** Wave 1 stays 25 tasks × 2 ratings, fuller-context judges only, new generators, and the frozen screen tested once. If the within-family correlation stays near 0.30, O1 will not pass no matter how many judges are added, and that negative is informative. What the cache cannot answer, and these ratings can, is whether H2 and the other fuller-context judges agree with people on 2026 candidate files (O2). The square-root sample-size projection in § 5.1 is retired as the planning cross-check; experiment 49's measured grid replaces it (§ 5.1a). The C15 bunched-file simulation is unchanged, because experiment 49 resampled the May files.

The partial freeze of 10-18-2026 locks the 25 wave-1 tasks and τ = 0.902 (balanced accuracy 0.971 on the 38 train and validation units; test units unread). It is in [freeze/FREEZE_10-18-2026.md](freeze/FREEZE_10-18-2026.md). Candidate bytes and the 51 judge selectors are not pinned, so this is not a start.

## 0. Terms used below

- **Judge:** a language model, with a prompt and a harness, that scores a candidate Lean file against a reference. **Rater** or **expert:** a person. **Rating:** the data on either side (never "label" or "judgment" in prose; frozen paths keep their old spelling).
- **FSC (formal specification coverage):** how much of the reference's theorem statements the candidate's theorem statements cover; experts rate 0–5, judges are normalized to [0, 1].
- **Judge–human agreement (the criterion):** for one judge configuration, the tie-aware Spearman correlation between its median-of-three score and the human target over the rated files, computed only when at least 90% of the declared files have a score (`experiments/39_…/scripts/analysis_lib.py`, `MIN_CRITERION_COVERAGE = 0.90`).
- **Screen / proxy:** a label-free number computed per judge from scripted edits of Lean files. **`C_ms_gold`** (gold missing-statement monotonicity): for each reference with a Lean-valid deletion ladder (levels 0–4, theorem statements removed cumulatively, last theorem first), the ladder quality (1 − ρ)/2 where ρ is the tie-aware Spearman between deletion level and the judge's median score (a constant ladder counts 0), averaged over ladders when at least 80% of them are scored (`analysis_lib.py` `ladder_quality`, `components`, `MIN_COMPONENT_COVERAGE = 0.80`). Selected in experiment 39 on the train judges, checked on the validation judges, tested once on the held-out judges (seal commit `86ae479201e5`).
- **`PTI_v0`** (the frozen four-property proxy, `docs/spec/PTI_V0.md`) and **`TI_var`** (the paper's earlier variance-aware index): the two pre-registered indices that did not transfer in experiment 39.
- **Outer association:** the Spearman correlation, across judge configurations, between a screen value and the criterion. **Inner interval:** the uncertainty of one judge's criterion (resampling tasks). **Outer interval:** the uncertainty of the outer association (resampling judge configurations).
- **ICC(A,1):** intraclass correlation, absolute-agreement form, one judge against the human target. **MAE:** mean absolute error. **Bias:** mean of (judge − human); positive means the judge is lenient. Both means are reported beside it.
- **VBV1:** the original VeriBench (896 Python-to-Lean tasks: 614 core + 282 IndustrySet). **Set N:** experiment 102's current rating set (25 core tasks × 5 Terminus 2 systems = 125 files, amendment C14). **A1:** Brando as a rater; never in a primary human target (C15 item 1).
- **Terminus 2:** Vals' agent harness used for every 2026 system in experiments 107 and 109. **Valkyrie:** Vals' evaluation runner; every judge call goes through it (`CLAUDE.md` § Valkyrie runs). **SNAP:** the Stanford Network Analysis Project cluster, where experiment 109's files live.
- **Fuller-context prompts:** D0 (`D0_deployed_tc_v1`), R4 (`R4_exp75_human_precondition`) and the human-mirror family H0/H1/H2 (experiment 100 `prompts.py`), which show the judge the whole reference. **Legacy prompts:** P1, P4, P8 and the lexical LEX, which show only the reference's last theorem block (`experiments/39_…/scripts/legacy.py`, `last_theorem_block`, lines 29–40).
- **IC_tests:** VeriBench's factor that is 1 when the agent's Lean file compiles and states at least one `example`; used here only as a compile-rate proxy.

## 1. The question, in one line

Does a label-free screen selected on existing judges (`C_ms_gold`) predict human agreement for new judges and for new candidate generators, measured on fresh ratings it never saw?

**What would count as an answer** (Trigger Rule 50: the consequential uncertainty). Experiment 39's PASS (test Spearman 0.558 [0.078, 0.762], one-sided permutation p-val=0.0127, 16 of 18 declared held-out configurations; `experiments/39_…/results/TEST_RECEIPT.json`) is conditional in three ways that no existing data can remove:

1. **Prompt groups.** The pooled association partly reflects the legacy-versus-fuller-context rendering split: without the lexical prompt it is 0.453 [−0.177, 0.825], p-val=0.0551 (14 units); on the 10 fuller-context units alone it is −0.371 [−0.787, 0.304], p-val=0.8615; the human-mirror rubric H2 alone has 3 measured test configurations, below the eight-unit minimum (`docs/project_assessment_10-05-2026.md`, recomputed byte-identically in `docs/PAPER_FRAMING_DECISION_10-13-2026.md` on branch `paper/framing-10-13-2026`).
2. **One generator.** The 45 criterion items are three temperature-0.7 samples of `claude-3-7-sonnet-20250219` per task, of which 3 of 75 elaborate under Lean 4.22 (`experiments/39_…/results.md`, deviations), so the candidate-side probe components were nearly empty and the family reduced to gold-side indices.
3. **Reused ratings.** The 75 ratings have been read by every cert-judge and VeriBench judge study since May 2026 (experiment 45 `PROPOSED_METHODS.md`; VeriBench `human_round/README.md` "The old ratings are used up"); experiment 44 found no selection advantage over a fixed human-mirror prompt on them (proxy 0.768 vs H2 0.853, paired difference −0.086 [−0.308, 0.058], p-val=n/a, 42 files / 15 tasks).

The test below removes all three at once: fuller-context rendering only, candidate files from eight 2026 agents in one harness, and ratings that no judge study has read.

## 4. Frozen outputs, each with its threshold and the threshold's source

Every statistic carries a 95% interval written `x [lo, hi] p-val=Z` with the test named, or `p-val=n/a` (Hard Rule 11). Draws 10,000, seed 48, fixed at the freeze.

### O1. Ranking of judges by human agreement (primary)

- **Statistic.** Over the 51 new units: Spearman between `C_ms_gold` computed on the pool's 25 reference ladders and each unit's criterion (its Spearman with the human target over the pool's files); Kendall τ-b and Pearson beside it.
- **Gate (PASS / FAIL / INCONCLUSIVE).** PASS requires all three: outer Spearman ≥ 0.45 **and** one-sided positive-association permutation p < 0.05 (10,000 permutations; source: experiment 39 `PROTOCOL.md`, test gates) **and** the configuration-bootstrap 95% lower bound > 0.20 (source: experiment 100's judge–human gate as restated in VeriBench `experiments/102_…/PROTOCOL.md` C5, "Spearman ≥ 0.45 with bootstrap lower bound > 0.20"; applying it to the outer association is this proposal's addition, after the 10-05 assessment's point that 39's permutation test "does not test that the population correlation exceeds 0.45" and its lower bound was 0.078). FAIL if any of the three fails. INCONCLUSIVE if the criterion or the index is NOT_COMPUTABLE for more than 14 of the 51 units (39's 5-of-18 proportion) or the bootstrap interval spans more than 1.2 (39's rule); never resolved by moving a threshold.
- **Intervals.** Unit bootstrap over judge configurations (comparable with 39's `TEST_RECEIPT.json`) is primary. Because the 51 units cluster in nine models, a model-cluster bootstrap and a leave-one-model-out table are declared sensitivities; 39 reported neither (10-05 assessment, "does not account for shared model/prompt clusters").
- **Secondary, same table:** `PTI_v0` (frozen, `docs/spec/PTI_V0.md`) and `TI_var` (train-normalized as in 39) on the same units; the other nine components and the pre-registered candidate family of `analysis_lib.CANDIDATES` as descriptive rows; the known-judge arm (34) and the all-units view (85) with the same gate applied descriptively.

### O2. Absolute agreement (co-primary for level claims)

- **Per judge, on the pool:** ICC(A,1) with ICC(C,1) (the consistency form, blind to a constant offset; the gap between the two is the offset) beside it, MAE, mean bias (judge − human, positive = lenient) with both means, repeat standard deviation over the three repeats; task-bootstrap intervals over the 25 folders. Level gates per judge, reported not screened: MAE ≤ 0.15, |bias| ≤ 0.10, ICC(A,1) ≥ 0.40 when the human target's standard deviation is at least 0.10 (source: VeriBench `PROTOCOL_AMENDMENT_C7.md`, for systems with at least 25 items; here each judge has up to 125 files).
- **Outer association with level:** Spearman across the 51 new units between `C_ms_gold` and each unit's MAE (expected negative) and |bias|, with unit-bootstrap intervals, **p-val=n/a and no gate**: no prior receipt links the screen to level agreement (experiment 44's single observation: the proxy-selected judge had MAE 0.154, bias −0.010 on means 0.493 vs 0.502, against H2's MAE 0.173, bias +0.120 on 0.623 vs 0.502; `experiments/archive/44_…/RESULTS_selection_utility.md`).
- **Human reliability on the pool:** leave-one-rater-out agreement, ICC(1,1) and ICC(1,2) (the one-way intraclass correlations for a single rating and for the mean of two, used because raters differ by file; C15 item 7.3), and the judge-versus-second-human reading of C15 item 7.1 (Δ = ρ_JH − ρ_HH with its interval; lower bound ≥ −0.10 reads "ranks about as well as a second qualified rater"). This is what separates "the judges are weak" from "these files are hard to rank" if ratings bunch.

### O3. Accept/reject screen (primary for the deployment question)

- **Threshold τ, fixed before any rating.** Zero-call, from existing receipts: over experiment 39's 38 train + validation units (`results/units_train_val.json`), τ is the `C_ms_gold` value maximizing balanced accuracy for "criterion ≥ 0.45"; the value and the script are committed in the freeze (§ 7). The test units of 39 are not used to set τ.
- **Truth per judge on the pool:** "meets the human gate" = criterion ≥ 0.45 with task-bootstrap lower bound > 0.20 (C5's rule).
- **False-acceptance rate:** among new units with `C_ms_gold` ≥ τ, the share that fails the human gate, with a unit-bootstrap interval; the unscreened base rate (share of all new units failing the gate) beside it.
- **Decision rule (agent-suggested, relative, no fixed constant):** the screen is useful for acceptance if the accepted set's failure rate is below the base rate with a paired unit-bootstrap 95% lower bound of (base − accepted) > 0; otherwise "not shown". The absolute false-acceptance rate is reported with its interval whatever the verdict; a reader who needs a fixed ceiling can read it against that number.
- **Selection loss (experiment 44's definitions, `RESULTS_selection_utility.md`):** the expected criterion of the proxy-selected judge (uniform over exact ties) minus the best judge's (regret), minus uniform selection among the H2 configurations (fixed human-mirror rubric; not an ensemble), and minus random selection; paired task-bootstrap intervals (resampling the 25 folders, fixed pool). Proxy selection is "useful" only if proxy − H2 has a positive lower 95% bound (source: experiment 45 `PROTOCOL.md`, "Question and decision criterion"); an interval across zero is "unresolved", not equivalence. Experiment 44's values on the reused panel, for comparison: proxy 0.768 [0.401, 0.930], H2 0.853 [0.645, 0.931], difference −0.086 [−0.308, 0.058], regret 0.104 [0.008, 0.379] vs 0.018 [0.009, 0.116], all p-val=n/a.

### O4. Reported but not gated

Per-prompt and per-harness views; the candidate-side components' coverage (how many of the 125 candidates elaborate and carry probes, against 39's 3 of 75); the four-rater-group views of C15 item 7.4 (team, Vals, non-qualified, A1 sensitivity); the semantic screen of experiment 42 recomputed on the pool's references only if its probe builder runs unchanged (otherwise "not run"); wave 2, if rated, as the pooled 48-task result beside wave 1.

### 5.2 Outer: the screen's association across judges, driven by the number of judge units

- **Measured:** experiment 39's 16-unit test interval, 0.558 [0.08, 0.76], spans **0.68** (`results.md` § Verdict). Experiment 45's five-rater recomputation on the same 16 units, 0.555 [0.420, 0.786], spans 0.37, but that interval resamples tasks with the judge panel fixed, so it is not an outer interval (`experiments/archive/45_…/RESULTS_five_rater_comparison.md`).
- **Arithmetic (Bonett–Wright large-sample approximation for Spearman, labelled an approximation, not a measurement):** the Fisher-z half-width is 1.96 × √((1 + ρ²/2)/(n − 3)). At ρ = 0.558 (z = 0.630; 1 + ρ²/2 = 1.156):
  - n = 16: 1.96 × √(1.156/13) = 1.96 × 0.298 = 0.585 → z ∈ [0.045, 1.215] → ρ ∈ [0.045, 0.838], width **0.79** (the measured bootstrap gave 0.68, so the approximation is slightly conservative here);
  - n = 51 (the new units): 1.96 × √(1.156/48) = 1.96 × 0.155 = 0.304 → z ∈ [0.326, 0.934] → ρ ∈ [0.315, 0.732], width **0.42**;
  - n = 85 (all units): 1.96 × √(1.156/82) = 1.96 × 0.119 = 0.233 → z ∈ [0.397, 0.863] → ρ ∈ [0.377, 0.698], width **0.32**.
- **Reading.** With 51 new units, O1's lower-bound rule (> 0.20) is met only if the true outer association is about 0.45 or higher; at 0.558 the expected lower bound is about 0.3. Units share nine models, so the effective count is below 51 and the model-cluster sensitivity (§ 4, O1) will be wider; this is why all nine registry families are declared rather than four.

- **Fuller-context power, measured (experiment 49, Q2).** The width of 0.42 above assumes a true correlation of 0.558, the mixed-family result. Inside fuller-context prompts the pool Spearman is 0.302 (32 configurations). Empirical power of the 0.45 gate there is 0.29 at 16 judges and 0.14 at 48 (1,000 draws × 1,000 permutations); the parametric curve at a true correlation of 0.30 falls from 0.23 at 16 judges to 0.09 at 48. The mixed-family pool reaches power 0.915 at 12 judges, and that figure is optimistic because the pool includes the train and validation judges the screen was selected on (pool Spearman 0.763). Adding judges does not power O1 inside the family this pool actually runs. The 51 units stay, because they are the new judges the screen has not seen, not because 51 is the number that makes a 0.30 correlation pass 0.45.


### 5.3 Why 25 tasks, 5 files and 51 judge units, in one line each

- Tasks: experiment 49 measures ranking reliability 0.965 and gate agreement 0.930 at 25 tasks × 2 raters on the historical files (§ 5.1a), which is why wave 1 stays at 25 rather than growing to power the inner criterion; 50 tasks remains the declared extension if the C15 bunched scenario shows up, not as a default.
- Files: five generators per task match set N's folder shape and include two lower-compile systems for spread (§ 2.2).
- Judge units: 51 is the list of new judges the screen has not seen. It is not a sample size that powers O1 inside fuller-context prompts (§ 5.2); every declared subset except the agent-judge and per-model views still clears the eight-unit minimum.

## 8. The two ways it can fail, and what each means for the paper

1. **The screen does not transfer (O1 FAIL on the 51 new units).** Then the label-free screen has no prospective support on new judges with matched information access, and experiment 39's PASS stands as a result conditional on its panel and its prompt groups. For the paper this is the decisive negative: the "rule out, not pick" story collapses to "a label-free screen can be selected that fits one panel, but it did not predict agreement for new judges on new generators" — a methods-plus-negative-result paper (TMLR, Transactions on Machine Learning Research, accepts it, `docs/PAPER_FRAMING_DECISION_10-13-2026.md` § Venue), with the fresh ratings still valuable on their own: they validate or refute every one of the 85 judges on frontier outputs (O2), which VeriBench's paper also needs. A FAIL with a wide interval (lower bound below 0.20 but point estimate above 0.45) is reported as exactly that, not as a negative.
2. **The fresh ratings cannot carry the test (O1 INCONCLUSIVE, or O2's human–human reading collapses).** Two forms: (a) ratings bunch near full coverage, so human–human agreement is itself about 0.3 (the C15 "bunched" scenario) and rank gates fail for every judge, including those that match a second human; (b) coverage is short (fewer than 50 pledged slots, qualified raters dropping out, or more than 14 units NOT_COMPUTABLE). In form (a) the paper can make no ranking claim either way on this pool; the absolute-agreement statistics and the second-human reading are what remain, and the next pool needs more spread (harder tasks, VeriBench V2, or weaker generators) rather than more files. In form (b) the result is reported as INCONCLUSIVE with its counts, the one-rating files are shown separately, and wave 2 is the recovery, not a re-draw.

A third, smaller outcome is worth naming: O1 PASS but O3 "not shown" (the screen ranks judges but accepts bad ones at the base rate). The paper may then claim ranking transfer and must say the screen is not a deployment gate; the 10-05 assessment already asks for exactly this separation.
