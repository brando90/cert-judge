# LLM Judges for Evaluating AI-Generated Code (2023–2026)

Scope: whether and how LLM-as-a-judge is used to evaluate generated code, and how central it is relative to execution-based evaluation (unit tests, pass@k). Purpose: support or refute the ICLR 2027 abstract opener "LLM judges are increasingly central to evaluating AI-generated code."

Verification method (2026-09-22): every arXiv ID below was resolved through the arXiv export API (title, authors, first-submission date, author comments). Venues come from the arXiv author comment where one exists, otherwise from the Semantic Scholar record (marked "per S2"). Numbers come from abstracts or from full text fetched from arxiv.org/html. Anything not confirmed this way is marked UNVERIFIED.

**Bottom line for the abstract sentence:** "increasingly" is well supported. "central" overstates the case if it is read as "more central than tests". Execution-based checks are still the headline standard for benchmarks and for RL rewards. LLM judges are central in specific places: code review, non-functional quality, reference-free or test-free settings, and choosing among candidate agent solutions. The safer wording is below under Q4 Inferences.

## Q1. Which papers propose or benchmark LLM judges for code? (authors, year, venue, arXiv id, one-line finding)

### Takeaway
There is a dense, fast-growing literature (2023→2026) of LLM-as-judge methods and judge benchmarks for code. It runs from prompt-based judges (ICE-Score, CodeJudge) to judge benchmarks (CodeJudge-Eval, CodeJudgeBench, CodeCriticBench), empirical human-alignment studies (ISSTA 2025, TSE 2025), and bias studies (EACL 2026 Findings). The consistent finding is that judges correlate with humans and with functional correctness better than BLEU-style metrics. They are still unreliable: they frequently misjudge correctness, depend on the order of responses, and are fooled by superficial changes to the code.

### Cited Findings

#### Judge methods (proposers)
- **ICE-Score**: Terry Yue Zhuo (sole author). arXiv 2304.14317 (Apr 2023). Findings of EACL 2024. It instructs LLMs to score code without test oracles or references. The paper claims "superior correlations with functional correctness and human preferences" across four programming languages. Its motivation: BLEU-type metrics correlate weakly with practitioners, and "human-written test suites... can be challenging in domains with low resources." — [arXiv 2304.14317](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14317)
- **CodeJudge**: **Weixi Tong, Tianyi Zhang** (2 authors). arXiv 2410.02184 (Oct 2024). **EMNLP 2024 Main (long)**. An LLM framework that evaluates the "semantic correctness of generated code without the need for test cases," using "slow thinking" prompting. Tested with 4 evaluator LLMs, 4 datasets and 5 languages. It beats existing methods in most settings, and with Llama-3-8B-Instruct it beats a SOTA GPT-3.5-based evaluator. — [arXiv 2410.02184](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02184)
- **CodeUltraFeedback**: Martin Weyssow, Aton Kamanda, Xin Zhou, Houari Sahraoui. arXiv 2403.09032 (Mar 2024). ACM TOSEM (vol. 35 per S2). The dataset has 10,000 coding instructions, each with 4 responses drawn from a pool of 14 LLMs. GPT-3.5 acts as judge and ranks them on 5 coding preferences. The judged data is then used for SFT plus DPO (RLAIF) on CodeLlama-7B-Instruct. Motivation: automated metrics and static analysis "often fail to capture the nuances of user instructions." — [arXiv 2403.09032](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.09032); venue [S2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/arxiv/2403.09032)
- **MCTS-Judge**: Yutong Wang, Pengliang Ji, Chaoqun Yang, Kaixin Li et al. (7 authors). arXiv 2502.12468 (Feb 2025). arXiv-only per S2. It applies test-time search (MCTS) to judging code correctness. Base-model accuracy rises from 41% to 80%, surpassing o1-series models with 3x fewer tokens. — [arXiv 2502.12468](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12468)
- **CodeVisionary**: Xinchen Wang, Pengfei Gao, Chao Peng, Ruida Hu et al. arXiv 2504.13472 (Apr 2025). ASE 2025 per S2, where it is retitled "An Agent-based Evaluation Framework for Complex Code Generation". An agent-based judge evaluated on a new 363-sample benchmark covering 37 coding scenarios and 23 languages. It argues that human-centered evaluation is labor-intensive and metric-based evaluation "overly rel[ies] on reference answers." — [arXiv 2504.13472](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13472); venue [S2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/arxiv/2504.13472)
- **SE-Jury (LLM-as-Ensemble-Judge)**: Xin Zhou, Kisub Kim, Ting Zhang, Martin Weyssow et al. (9 authors). arXiv 2505.20854 (May 2025). arXiv-only per S2. An ensemble of five judge strategies covering code generation, program repair and summarization. Correlation with humans improves by 29.6%–140.8% over existing automatic metrics, and agreement is "close to inter-annotator agreement." — [arXiv 2505.20854](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.20854)
- **Agent-as-a-Judge**: Mingchen Zhuge, Changsheng Zhao, Dylan Ashley, Wenyi Wang et al. (13 authors). arXiv 2410.10934 (Oct 2024). ICML 2025 per S2. It uses agentic judges on code-generation tasks, with the DevAI benchmark of 55 AI-development tasks and 365 hierarchical requirements. Agent-as-a-Judge "dramatically outperforms LLM-as-a-Judge and is as reliable as our human evaluation baseline." — [arXiv 2410.10934](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10934)
- **LLM Critics for Execution-Free Evaluation of Code Changes (Amazon)**: Aashish Yadavally, Hoan Nguyen, Laurent Callot, Gauthier Guinet. arXiv 2501.16655 (Jan 2025). arXiv-only per S2. Reference-aware LLM critics, given the gold test patch, predict executability of edit locations with F1 91.6% and SWE-bench build status in 84.8% of instances. They outperform other critics by 38.9%–72.5%. — [arXiv 2501.16655](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16655)
- **CODERPE (code-summarization judge)**: Yang Wu, Yao Wan, Zhaoyang Chu, Wenting Zhao et al. arXiv 2412.01333 (Dec 2024). IEEE TSE vol. 51 per S2. Role-play LLM evaluation of code summaries reaches 81.59% Spearman correlation with human evaluation. — [arXiv 2412.01333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.01333); venue [S2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/arxiv/2412.01333)
- **CRScore (code-review-comment metric)**: Atharva Naik, Marcus Alenius, Daniel Fried, Carolyn Rose. arXiv 2409.19801 (Sep 2024). NAACL (2025) per S2. A reference-free review-quality metric grounded in claims from LLMs and static analyzers. It reaches 0.54 Spearman with humans and releases 2.9k human-annotated review-quality scores. — [arXiv 2409.19801](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.19801)

#### Judge benchmarks and empirical studies (does the judge work?)
- **CodeJudge-Eval (CJ-Eval)**: Yuwei Zhao, Ziyang Luo, Yuchen Tian, Hongzhan Lin et al. (7 authors). arXiv 2408.10718 (Aug 2024). COLING per S2 (S2 year field says 2024; the conference was COLING 2025). Models must judge whether provided solutions are correct. Across 12 LLMs, "even state-of-the-art models struggle." — [arXiv 2408.10718](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.10718); venue [S2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/arxiv/2408.10718)
- **CodeJudgeBench**: Hongchao Jiang, Yiming Chen, Yushi Cao, Hung-yi Lee et al. (5 authors). arXiv 2507.10535 (Jul 2025). S2 lists ACL, but the track and year are UNVERIFIED. It benchmarks 26 judge models on code generation, repair and unit-test generation. Thinking models win (Qwen3-8B beats specially trained judges up to 70B), but "all models still exhibit significant randomness," and swapping the order of pairwise responses "can substantially impact accuracy." — [arXiv 2507.10535](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.10535); venue [S2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/arxiv/2507.10535)
- **"Can LLMs Replace Human Evaluators? An Empirical Study of LLM-as-a-Judge in Software Engineering"**: Ruiqi Wang, Jiyu Guo, Cuiyun Gao, Guodong Fan et al. (6 authors). arXiv 2502.06193 (Feb 2025). ISSTA 2025, published in Proc. ACM Softw. Eng. vol. 2. It tests 7 general-purpose judge methods and 2 fine-tuned evaluators against human scores on code translation, generation and summarization. Output-based methods reach Pearson 81.32 (translation) and 68.51 (generation), against 34.23 and 64.92 for ChrF++. — [arXiv 2502.06193](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06193)
- **"On the Effectiveness of LLM-as-a-judge for Code Generation and Summarization"**: Giuseppe Crupi, Rosalia Tufano, Alejandro Velasco, Antonio Mastropaolo et al. (6 authors). arXiv 2507.16587 (Jul 2025). IEEE TSE (vol. 51). Eight LLMs judge the correctness of 1,405 Java methods and 1,281 Python functions. GPT-4-turbo is best, and smaller LLMs "not being able to cope," but "even the best-performing LLM frequently misjudges the correctness of the code." — [arXiv 2507.16587](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.16587)
- **"Don't Judge Code by Its Cover: Exploring Biases in LLM Judges for Code Evaluation"**: Jiwon Moon, Yerin Hwang, Dongryeol Lee, Taegwan Kang et al. (6 authors). arXiv 2505.16222 (May 2025). Findings of EACL 2026. It defines 6 bias types, such as variable names, comments and formatting, and tests 5 languages. "All tested LLM judges are susceptible to both positive and negative biases," even when prompted to generate test cases before scoring. This is directly relevant to the alpha-renaming invariance property (P_eqalpha). — [arXiv 2505.16222](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.16222)
- **CodeCriticBench**: Alexander Zhang, Marcus Dong, Jiaheng Liu, Wei Zhang et al. (18 authors). arXiv 2502.16614 (Feb 2025). arXiv-only per S2. A code-critique benchmark covering generation and code QA, with checklists for fine-grained critique evaluation. It argues that earlier critique benchmarks used easy HumanEval/MBPP queries. — [arXiv 2502.16614](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.16614)
- **CodeArena (Evaluating and Aligning CodeLLMs on Human Preference)**: Jian Yang, Jiaxi Yang, Ke Jin, Yibo Miao et al. (10 authors). arXiv 2412.05210 (Dec 2024). arXiv-only per S2. It has 397 human-curated samples across 40 categories and 44 languages, and "find[s] performance differences between execution-based benchmarks and CodeArena." (The exact judge model is UNVERIFIED because the abstract was truncated.) — [arXiv 2412.05210](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.05210)
- **LLM Critics Help Catch LLM Bugs (CriticGPT, OpenAI)**: Nat McAleese, Rai Michael Pokorny, Juan Felipe Ceron Uribe, Evgenia Nitishinskaya et al. (6 authors). arXiv 2407.00215 (Jun 2024). Technical report. RLHF-trained critics of model-written code are preferred over human critiques in 63% of cases, and they "catch more bugs than human contractors paid for code review," but they hallucinate bugs. — [arXiv 2407.00215](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.00215)

#### SE-specific LLM-as-judge surveys
- **"From Code to Courtroom: LLMs as the New Software Judges"**: Junda He, Jieke Shi, Terry Yue Zhuo, Christoph Treude et al. (8 authors). arXiv 2503.02246 (Mar 2025). An SE 2030 vision paper reviewing **16 primary studies**. — [arXiv 2503.02246 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.02246)

### Inferences
- Judge accuracy on code correctness is improving (thinking models, MCTS-Judge, agentic judges), but independent studies (TSE 2025, EACL 2026 Findings, CodeJudgeBench) agree on three reliability problems: frequent misjudgment, sensitivity to response order, and sensitivity to surface changes. This is the gap PTI-style label-free behavioral probes address.
- The bias results in Moon et al. (renaming, comments, formatting) map almost one-to-one onto the PTI-v0 alpha-renaming invariance property. It is the strongest external citation for why P_eqalpha matters.
- Candidate new bib entries for the related-work paragraph: CodeJudgeBench, CodeJudge-Eval, Wang et al. ISSTA 2025, Crupi et al. TSE 2025, Moon et al. EACL 2026, He et al. 2510.24367.

## Q2. Where are LLM judges used in practice for code? (code review, SWE-bench-style grading, RL rewards, industry pipelines)

### Takeaway
In practice, LLM judges are most entrenched in **code review**: GitHub Copilot, OpenAI Codex, Anthropic Claude Code, Google AutoCommenter and Qodo PR-Agent. As of September 2026, GitHub lets Copilot *sign off on PR approval*. In **agentic-coding and RL pipelines**, execution-free LLM verifiers and reward models are now used alongside tests, mainly for test-time selection and increasingly for RL (SWE-Gym, R2E-Gym, SWE-RM). The headline **benchmarks and RL rewards remain execution-based**: SWE-bench, DeepSeek-R1, AceCoder and SWE-RL all use tests or rules. Judge-based grading appears in benchmarks where there are no tests (PaperBench, DevAI).

### Cited Findings

#### Code review (industry)
- GitHub (changelog, 2026-09-01): "Copilot now tells you when a pull request is ready to approve, and admins can authorize it to sign off on approval." The capability is "off by default," in public preview for Copilot Pro, Pro+, Max, Business and Enterprise. The changelog gives no usage statistics. — [GitHub Changelog 2026-09-01](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-09-01-copilot-code-review-can-now-approve-pull-requests/)
- GitHub (changelog, 2025-12-18): Copilot code review preview features add "agentic tool calling" to gather project context, plus configurable deterministic tools "like CodeQL and ESLint." This is a hybrid of a judge and static analysis. Source: a search-result summary; the page itself was not fetched. — [GitHub Changelog 2025-12-18](https://github.blog/changelog/2025-12-18-copilot-code-review-preview-features-now-supported-in-github-enterprise-cloud-with-data-residency/)
- OpenAI: GPT-5-Codex "has been trained specifically for conducting code reviews and finding critical flaws." When reviewing, it "runs your code and tests in order to validate correctness," and it is "the default for cloud tasks and code review." This is from a search-result snippet of OpenAI's post; a direct fetch returned HTTP 403. Any claim about what share of OpenAI's internal PRs Codex reviews is **UNVERIFIED**. — [OpenAI, "Introducing upgrades to Codex"](https://openai.com/index/introducing-upgrades-to-codex/)
- Anthropic launched "Code Review" for Claude Code on 2026-03-09. It is "a multi-agent system that automatically analyzes AI-generated code, flags logic errors," in research preview for Claude for Teams and Enterprise, at an estimated "$15 to $25 on average" per review. The stated motivation (Cat Wu): "Now that Claude Code is putting up a bunch of pull requests, how do I make sure that those get reviewed in an efficient manner?" — [TechCrunch, 2026-03-09](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/09/anthropic-launches-code-review-tool-to-check-flood-of-ai-generated-code/)
  - Search summaries also reported that substantive-comment coverage rose from 16% to 54% of PRs and that under 1% of findings were marked incorrect. These figures are **UNVERIFIED**: the TechCrunch article does not contain them and no primary Anthropic source was fetched. The same summaries said availability was Pro/Max, while TechCrunch says Teams/Enterprise. Treat that as a conflict. — [InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/04/claude-code-review/) (not fetched)
- Google **AutoCommenter** (Vijayvergiya et al., arXiv 2405.13565, AIware '24) is an LLM-backed system that "automatically learns and enforces coding best practices" in modern code review for C++, Java, Python and Go. It was deployed in "a large industrial setting," with lessons from deploying it "to tens of thousands of developers." — [arXiv 2405.13565](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13565)
- **"Automated Code Review In Practice"** (Cihan et al., arXiv 2412.18531, ICSE-SEIP 2025) studied a tool based on open-source Qodo PR-Agent. About 238 practitioners had access. In 3 projects with 4,335 PRs, 1,568 received automated reviews and "73.8% of automated comments were resolved." However, average PR closure time rose from 5h52m to 8h20m. The paper names Qodo, GitHub Copilot and CodeRabbit as LLM review tools. — [arXiv 2412.18531](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.18531)

#### SWE-bench-style and agentic-coding grading
- **SWE-bench** (Jimenez et al., arXiv 2310.06770, ICLR 2024) has 2,294 real GitHub issues and is graded by executing tests. It is the canonical execution-based agentic benchmark. — [arXiv 2310.06770](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06770)
- **SWE-Lancer** (Miserendino et al., arXiv 2502.12115, ICML 2025 per S2): "Independent tasks are graded with end-to-end tests triple-verified by experienced software engineers." Managerial tasks have models "choose between technical implementation proposals." Grading here is execution-based, not by an LLM judge. — [arXiv 2502.12115](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12115)
- **PaperBench** (Starace et al., OpenAI, arXiv 2504.01848, ICML 2025 per S2) is a frontier-lab benchmark in which an **LLM judge grades agent-written research code** against author-co-developed rubrics: 20 ICML 2024 papers and 8,316 gradable tasks. OpenAI built "a separate benchmark for judges." The best agent scored 21.0%. — [arXiv 2504.01848](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01848)
- **Agent-as-a-Judge / DevAI** (ICML 2025 per S2): judge-based grading of AI-development tasks where tests are absent. — [arXiv 2410.10934](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.10934)

#### RL and test-time rewards for code: execution vs LLM-judged
- **DeepSeek-R1** (DeepSeek-AI, arXiv 2501.12948; *Nature* vol. 645, 2025 per S2) uses execution for code rewards: "for code competition prompts, a compiler can be utilized to evaluate the model's responses against a suite of predefined test cases." It explicitly avoided neural reward models: "neural reward models are susceptible to reward hacking during large-scale reinforcement learning." The paper also states that "rule-based reward models (RMs) and LLMs to assess an answer's correctness against a predefined ground-truth—serve as robust mechanisms for mitigating... reward hacking." — [arXiv 2501.12948 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.12948)
- **SWE-RL** (Wei et al., Meta, arXiv 2502.18449, NeurIPS 2025) uses "a lightweight rule-based reward (e.g., the similarity score between ground-truth and LLM-generated solutions)." It reaches 41.0% on SWE-bench Verified. The reward is rule-based, not an LLM judge. — [arXiv 2502.18449](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.18449)
- **AceCoder** (Zeng et al., arXiv 2502.01718, ACL 2025 main) synthesizes test cases, trains a Bradley–Terry reward model on pass-rate preferences, and runs RL with both the RM and test-pass rewards. Its motivation: "the lack of reliable reward data/model in the code domain." — [arXiv 2502.01718](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01718)
- **CodeRM-8B** (Ma et al., arXiv 2501.01054, ACL 2025 per S2) observes that LLM-generated unit tests used as rewards "are not reliable" and scales them dynamically. It gains 18.43% for Llama3-8B on HumanEval Plus. — [arXiv 2501.01054](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.01054)
- **SWE-Gym** (Pan et al., arXiv 2412.21139, ICML 2025) trains **verifiers on agent trajectories** for inference-time scaling, reaching 32.0% and 26.0% on SWE-Bench Verified and Lite. — [arXiv 2412.21139](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.21139)
- **R2E-Gym** (Jain et al., arXiv 2504.07164) compares execution-based and execution-free verifiers. "Test-based verifiers suffer from low distinguishability, while execution-free verifiers are biased and often rely on stylistic features." Each saturates at about 42–43%, and the hybrid reaches 51% on SWE-Bench Verified. — [arXiv 2504.07164](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07164)
- **SWE-RM** (Shum, Hui, Chen et al., Qwen team, arXiv 2512.21919, Dec 2025) is an execution-free reward model (MoE, 30B total and 3B active) for both test-time scaling and RL. Test-time scaling lifts Qwen3-Coder-Flash from 51.6% to 62.0% and Qwen3-Coder-Max from 67.0% to 74.6% on SWE-Bench Verified. The paper notes that verifiers with near-identical test-time performance "can nevertheless yield very different results in RL." — [arXiv 2512.21919](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.21919)
- **CodeUltraFeedback** uses GPT-3.5 **as a judge to produce preference data** for DPO/RLAIF on code. — [arXiv 2403.09032](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.09032)
- **CriticGPT** trains critics with RLHF to help humans evaluate code, so it serves as a judge within RLHF pipelines. — [arXiv 2407.00215](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.00215)

#### Human-preference evaluation (not LLM judges; for contrast)
- **Copilot Arena** (Chi et al., arXiv 2502.09328, ICML 2025 per S2) collects in-IDE user preferences. — [arXiv 2502.09328](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.09328)
- **BigCodeArena** (Zhuo et al., arXiv 2510.08697) notes that "manually examining the quality of LLM-generated content is extremely challenging, as it requires understanding long chunks of raw code and deliberately simulating code execution." — [arXiv 2510.08697](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.08697)

### Inferences
- The clearest "central" role for LLM judges is **code review of AI-generated PRs**. Vendors present it as the answer to a review bottleneck created by AI code volume (TechCrunch quote). GitHub letting Copilot sign off on approval, even though off by default, means a judge's verdict can now gate merges.
- In RL, the frontier recipe (DeepSeek-R1) deliberately prefers execution and rule rewards over learned judges because of reward hacking. Execution-free LLM verifiers are used mainly for **test-time selection** and are only starting to be used for RL (SWE-RM, Dec 2025). "LLM-judged rewards replacing execution rewards" is therefore **not** the current norm. "LLM verifiers complementing execution" is.

### Gaps
- No primary source was retrieved for internal adoption rates, such as the share of OpenAI or Anthropic PRs reviewed by AI or the number of Copilot reviews. The OpenAI page returned 403 and the GitHub changelog has no statistics.
- CodeRabbit, Cursor Bugbot and Qodo usage figures were not investigated.
- Whether SWE-bench leaderboards or labs use LLM judges to *grade* (as opposed to *select*) was not found. All evidence indicates grading remains test-based.

## Q3. Why are LLM judges needed for code when tests exist?

### Takeaway
The literature gives five recurring reasons. (1) Tests are often **missing** or costly to build, needing environments and labor. (2) Tests are **insufficient**, so plausible-but-wrong code passes. (3) Tests are blind to **non-functional qualities**: readability, maintainability, efficiency, security, intent. (4) Reference-based metrics like BLEU correlate poorly with humans, while judges can work **reference-free**. (5) **Human** evaluation is too expensive to scale.

### Cited Findings
- **Missing or expensive tests**: pass@k "necessitates extensive unit tests and configured environments, demands a high labor cost, and is not suitable for evaluating LLM-generated text." — [Wang et al., ISSTA 2025, arXiv 2502.06193](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.06193)
- **Missing tests in low-resource domains**: "utilizing human-written test suites to evaluate functional correctness can be challenging in domains with low resources." — [Zhuo, ICE-Score, arXiv 2304.14317](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.14317)
- **Hard-to-test tasks**: "Constructing test cases can also be particularly challenging for certain tasks (Tong and Zhang, 2024), such as AI model training, web scraping," and "passing all the test cases does not necessarily mean the quality of the code is good." The review lists "Execution-free" as a defining property of LLM-as-a-Judge. — [He et al., arXiv 2510.24367 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24367)
- **Test-free correctness judging**: CodeJudge evaluates "semantic correctness of generated code without the need for test cases." — [Tong & Zhang, EMNLP 2024, arXiv 2410.02184](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02184)
- **Tests are insufficient (function level)**: EvalPlus extends HumanEval tests by 80x. This reduces pass@k by up to 19.3–28.9%, and "test insufficiency can lead to mis-ranking." (Liu et al., NeurIPS 2023 per S2.) — [arXiv 2305.01210](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01210)
- **Tests are insufficient (repository level)**: SWE-bench validation lets 7.8% of patches "count as correct while failing the developer-written test suite." Also, "29.6% plausible patches induce different behavior than the ground truth patches." (Wang, Pradel, Liu; ICSE 2026 per S2.) — [arXiv 2503.15223](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.15223)
- **Benchmark test quality is lagging**: a survey of 672 code benchmarks (2014–2025) finds that "in 2025 alone, the number of benchmarks that ignore code coverage when providing test cases nearly matches the total count accumulated across the previous ten years." — [Cao et al., arXiv 2501.10711](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.10711)
- **Build status is too sparse**: existing metrics for agentic code-change workflows, "mainly build status and occasionally log analysis, are too sparse and limited." — [Yadavally et al., arXiv 2501.16655](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16655)
- **Execution feedback is sparse for agents**: unit-test feedback "is often sparse and cannot effectively distinguish between trajectories that are both successful or both unsuccessful." — [SWE-RM, arXiv 2512.21919](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.21919)
- **Non-functional qualities**: benchmarks "focus almost exclusively on whether the LMs can generate functionally-correct code," while developers need "efficiency, security, and maintainability." (NoFunEval, COLM 2024.) — [arXiv 2401.15963](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.15963)
- **Beyond correctness**: current benchmarks "primarily assess the accuracy of LLM-generated code, while neglecting other critical dimensions." (RACE, Zheng et al.) — [arXiv 2407.11470](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.11470)
- **Human preference**: "current code evaluation remains anchored to pass@k and captures only functional correctness." (SWE-IF, Zhong et al., ICML 2026 per arXiv comment.) — [arXiv 2510.07315](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.07315)
- **Reference-based metrics and human cost**: "Human evaluation, while effective, is very costly and time-consuming. Traditional automated metrics like BLEU rely on high-quality references and struggle to capture nuanced aspects of software quality, such as readability and usefulness." — [He et al., arXiv 2503.02246](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.02246)
- **Metrics tell only part of the story**: judges are for tasks where "(i) quantitative metrics would only tell part of the story, and; (ii) a large-scale human-based evaluation would be too expensive." — [Crupi et al., TSE 2025, arXiv 2507.16587](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.16587)
- **Counter-evidence on execution-free verifiers**: they "are biased and often rely on stylistic features." — [R2E-Gym, arXiv 2504.07164](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07164)
- **Counter-evidence from frontier RL**: neural RMs invite reward hacking. — [DeepSeek-R1](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.12948)

### Inferences
- The argument transfers directly to Lean 4 specifications. A spec that compiles (or a theorem that checks) is the analogue of "passes tests." It says nothing about whether the spec is **faithful to intent**. That is exactly the gap where the literature says judges are needed and where their reliability is unproven. The paper can cite EvalPlus and Wang/Pradel/Liu for "passing the checker ≠ correct" and He et al. and NoFunEval for "properties tests cannot see."

### Gaps
- No paper was found that estimates what fraction of real-world code-generation tasks *lack* usable tests.

## Q4. Evidence on how often execution-based vs judge-based evaluation is used (surveys)

### Takeaway
No survey was found that gives a direct percentage split between execution-based and judge-based evaluation in code-generation papers. The available evidence is indirect. SE LLM-as-judge publications grew from 1 (2023) to 15 (2024) to 26 (2025, through August). Benchmark catalogs (291 SE benchmarks; 672 code benchmarks) show that pass@k and test execution still dominate code-generation benchmarks. The literature itself describes execution as the pre-judge default. The evidence supports "increasingly used"; it does not show "central" in the sense of displacing tests.

### Cited Findings
- **Growth of SE LLM-as-judge work**: He et al. did "a systematic review of 42 primary studies on the use of LLM-as-a-Judge in software engineering." "Following the first work published in 2023, the field expanded with 15 publications in 2024. This trend intensified remarkably in 2025, with 26 publications cataloged by August alone." — [He et al., arXiv 2510.24367 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24367)
- **Earlier count**: the March 2025 version reviewed 16 primary studies, so the count went from 16 to 42 in about 7 months. — [He et al., arXiv 2503.02246 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.02246)
- **Execution was the default**: "Before the introduction of LLM-as-a-Judge, automated code evaluation primarily relied on execution-based method, i.e., executing the code based on pre-defined test cases." — [He et al., arXiv 2510.24367 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.24367)
- **Benchmark catalogs**: Hu et al. (TOSEM 2025 per S2) review 291 SE benchmarks. Their tables list Pass@k (or Pass@1, n@k) as the metric for the code-generation benchmarks shown: HumanEval, miniF2F, Lyra, ReCode, NumpyEval, PandasEval and others. I found no aggregate percentage in the text I grepped. — [arXiv 2505.08903 (HTML)](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.08903)
- **Scale of benchmark building**: Cao et al. survey 672 code benchmarks (2014–2025) and focus on test and coverage quality. — [arXiv 2501.10711](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.10711)
- **Code-generation evaluation survey**: Chen et al. (arXiv 2408.16498) group evaluation into correctness, efficiency, readability, expert review and user experience. They analyze "code compilation/interpretation success rates, unit test pass rates, and performance and efficiency metrics." No LLM-as-judge share is reported in the abstract, and a grep of the HTML for judge terms returned nothing. — [arXiv 2408.16498](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.16498)
- **Critical review of metrics**: Paul, Zhu, Bayley review benchmarks and metrics for code generation (TELLMe 2024 workshop). — [arXiv 2406.12655](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.12655)
- **Code-generation survey**: Jiang et al., "A Survey on Large Language Models for Code Generation" (TOSEM vol. 35 per S2). It was not mined for judge-vs-execution statistics. — [arXiv 2406.00515](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.00515)
- **General LLM-as-judge surveys** that include code: Gu et al., "A Survey on LLM-as-a-Judge" (arXiv 2411.15594), and Li et al., "LLMs-as-Judges: A Comprehensive Survey" (arXiv 2412.05579). Their code-specific statistics were not extracted. — [arXiv 2411.15594](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15594); [arXiv 2412.05579](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.05579)
- **Leading benchmarks remain execution-based** (ICLR 2025 Oral; ICLR 2024; NeurIPS 2023): BigCodeBench, LiveCodeBench, SWE-bench and EvalPlus. — [BigCodeBench, arXiv 2406.15877](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.15877); [LiveCodeBench, arXiv 2403.07974](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.07974); [SWE-bench, arXiv 2310.06770](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06770); [EvalPlus, arXiv 2305.01210](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01210)

### Inferences
- **Verdict on the abstract claim.** "LLM judges are **increasingly** used / important" is supported by:
  - the publication growth (1 → 15 → 26);
  - industry code-review deployments (GitHub, OpenAI, Anthropic, Google);
  - LLM verifiers and reward models in agentic-coding pipelines (SWE-Gym, R2E-Gym, SWE-RM);
  - LLM-judged frontier benchmarks (PaperBench).
- "**Central**" is contestable if a reviewer reads it as central to *benchmarking* or *RL rewards*. There, execution still dominates, and DeepSeek-R1 explicitly avoids neural reward models.
- Suggested wording that stays within the evidence:
  - (a) "LLM judges are increasingly used to evaluate AI-generated code, especially where tests are missing, incomplete, or blind to the property of interest"
  - (b) "LLM judges are increasingly deployed alongside execution-based tests to review, rank, and reward AI-generated code"
  - (c) "…increasingly central to reviewing AI-generated code" (the review framing, backed by the GitHub, Anthropic and OpenAI sources)
- For the Lean-spec paper, a natural bridge: "…and to judging formal specifications, where type-checking certifies well-formedness but not fidelity."
- Minimal citation set for the opener: He et al. 2510.24367 (growth), Zhuo 2024 ICE-Score and Tong & Zhang 2024 CodeJudge (methods), Jiang et al. CodeJudgeBench or Wang et al. ISSTA 2025 (benchmarking), plus one deployment source (the GitHub changelog or Cihan et al. ICSE-SEIP 2025) if industry use is claimed.

### Gaps
- **No quantitative survey** was found that reports the fraction of code-generation papers or benchmarks using LLM judges versus execution. Such a count would require mining a survey's tables, for example Hu et al.'s 291 benchmarks or Cao et al.'s 672. Neither abstract reports a judge-based share.
- The review by He et al. covers SE broadly (code generation, summarization, repair, review), not code generation alone. Its counts cover papers *about* LLM-as-judge in SE, not papers that *use* a judge as their evaluation.
- No industry-wide survey data, such as a developer survey on AI code-review adoption, was retrieved.
