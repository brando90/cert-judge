**Title:** Author response: metadata rendering fault and scope of claims

Dear Editors-in-Chief,

Thank you for the assessment and for the transparency about reviewer capacity; we understand the constraints. We would like to note three factual points and ask one question.

**1. A metadata rendering fault (our mistake) misrepresents the paper's writing quality.** The abstract was entered into the submission form with LaTeX inline-math delimiters that OpenReview does not render, so the trust-index symbol was silently dropped: the landing-page abstract reads "a variance-aware trust index, ," and "On a human-labeled validation suite,  strongly predicts" — malformed in two places, alongside internal section references that belong only in the PDF. The manuscript's abstract is intact. If the preliminary assessment began from the form abstract, the submission's writing quality was misrepresented by our formatting error rather than by the manuscript itself.

**2. Claims and evidence are deliberately aligned, including the negative components.** The paper's headline results are explicitly scoped as validation-set calibration evidence (n = 8 judge configurations, reported with bootstrap confidence intervals and permutation p-values). Rather than omitting the planned held-out test, the submission discloses its outcome: an annotation-protocol revision confounds the direct test (cross-protocol label correlation ≈ 0.13), and a pre-registered 32-configuration screen returned its registered negative for label-free transfer. We believe this is the kind of claims–evidence alignment — including disclosed negative results — that TMLR's first acceptance criterion asks for.

**3. Desk-check compliance.** The submission is anonymized, uses the unmodified TMLR stylefile, contains 11 pages of main content, and its only prior appearance is a publicly declared non-archival workshop, which TMLR's editorial policy permits.

Our question: in light of point 1 in particular, would the EiCs be willing to take a second look — or alternatively, could you advise whether a revised resubmission (corrected metadata; exposition restructured around the certification-protocol contribution and the pre-registered transfer analysis) would be appropriate under the resubmission policy?

Respectfully,
The authors
