A gold theorem is covered only if the agent explicitly proves or asserts the exact statement of the theorem, not just defines components or provides examples. Coverage requires independent validation of the theorem's conclusion, not just related properties or test cases. Theorems must be fully stated, justified, and not rely on runtime checks or unproven definitions. Partially defined or empty theorems receive low scores. Precondition exclusions or repetitive computations without new properties disqualify coverage. Only theorems that establish universal guarantees, not just specific instances or restatements, are considered covered.
